# Design basis

The decisions taken so far, with enough of the reasoning and numbers to judge them. The detail
and sources are in [`research/`](research/). The decisions were made in the private Hailey repo,
whose issue numbers appear in the research notes as `XDGFX/hailey#NNN`.

Prices are USD from JLCPCB/LCSC on 2026-10-09, at 10–50 quantity.

## Requirements

- Measure the current on every way of a 12-way blade fuse panel. Every way gets a sensor,
  spare ways included.
- Read every way's voltage too, so a blown or pulled fuse can raise an alert.
- About ±0.3 A is good enough. Very small loads reading as zero is accepted.
- Don't limit the panel: 30 A per way, 100 A per panel.
- Existing #8 / M4 ring terminals must fit without re-crimping. The panel cover must still fit.
- Report to Home Assistant over Wi-Fi via ESPHome.
- Accept 12 V and 24 V systems.
- Cheap, and fully assembled by JLCPCB, with no hand soldering.

## Decisions

| Area | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Topology | One yoke per 12-way ST Blade: a single board for all 12 ways, with two arms of six fingers joined by a chin past the panel's positive end. The chin carries the one ESP32, supply, ground nut and programming pads; the arms carry only each way's own parts and are 16.7 mm deep. So far it is an outline, 117.6 × 122.3 mm, on [`explore/yoke`](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/tree/explore/yoke); its schematic, placement and routing are still to do. This replaces identical standalone 6-way strips, two per panel. Every row below still describes that strip, rev A, which stays order-ready and is not ordered; each row holds until the yoke's layout revises it. Whether the strip is kept for 6-way panels is open | Cost is close to even: $1.65 less for two panels on a 5-board, 2-assembled order and $20.25 more for one (2026-10-10). The yoke is 22.6 mm narrower on the bulkhead, is one Home Assistant device reading 12 ways on one clock, can number its ways in silk and firmware, and keeps its silkscreen in view once wired. Accepted costs: all 12 way screws out and the feed lug off to fit it, one module failing loses 12 ways, and it fits only a 12-way ST Blade. The strip's case rested on cable cost between strips, which one board doesn't have, and on about 1 W more standby, which the datasheets put at 0.09–0.33 W. Still to check with a printed plate before ordering: the feed lug passing over the chin, the cover and a clone ([note](research/yoke-or-strips.md), [#40](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/40)) |
| Mechanical | A comb. Each finger, 9.5 mm wide, slides sideways into a way's pocket at screw-pad height and is clamped under the panel's own #8 screw, through a 5.4 mm round hole. The strip sits outside the panel edge and needs 25.5 mm of space beside it. Parts face down, ring face up, so rings are screwed on from above. Outline: [comb geometry](assets/comb-outline-rev-a.svg) | Barriers stand 4–8.5 mm above the screw pads, so a flat board can't lie under all six screws. A 1.6 mm finger leaves about 4 mm of thread engaged ([dimensions](research/fuse-panel-dimensions.md)). 9.5 mm and 5.4 mm also fit the Blue Sea clones: a 1.6 mm printed plate of the routed board fits both a real ST Blade and a Blue Sea clone ([#13](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/13)). A hole rather than an open slot, because the old rings have to come off the screws anyway; the hole keeps the tip solid and the strip captive ([#8](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/8)) |
| Fixing | The six way screws are the only required fixing. The body has no fixing holes. An optional 3D-printed support, screwed to the bulkhead, can slide over the edge of the board to carry the body. Install notes: fit the strip and torque all six way screws first, keeping the panel's own lock washer under each, then fit the output rings with an M4 split spring washer under each bolt (Bunnings: Pinnacle M4 stainless spring washer, 12 pack, item 0851291, AUD 2.63 on 2026-10-10; 4 mm bore, 8 mm OD, so it sits inside the ring); tie output cables to the bulkhead within about 100 mm. Lead the main feed cable straight out off the positive end or straight up from the + stud, never across or along a strip. Re-check every torque after the first warm run. On the first boards, check the torque after a hot soak at load | Both joints clamp FR4, which creeps under load when warm, so the bolts lose preload over heat cycles, and a loose 30 A joint heats itself further. A spring washer keeps the clamp up while the board settles. A feed cable laid sideways over a strip's positive end is estimated to move way 1's reading by about 0.045 A per 100 A, against 0.006 A or less led straight out or up; the estimate is unmeasured until the first built strip is bench-checked ([#57](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/57), [#62](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/62)). A conical (DIN 6796) washer would hold it better, but neither Bunnings nor Jaycar stocks one, and at 9 mm OD the M4 size only just fits the ring; the panel's CAD already shows a lock washer on every screw ([dimensions](research/fuse-panel-dimensions.md)). Six clamped screws hold the strip in the panel's plane with a wide margin, and the round holes keep it captive while any screw is in. The weak case is the body bending. Pushing down to drive an output bolt or pulling a cable puts about 2–4 N·m on the finger roots, roughly 80–160 MPa against FR4's roughly 400 MPa. No SMT standoff comes in the 14.3 mm needed, and they are extended parts (about $3.30–3.60). M3 holes with 12 × 12 mm keep-clear zones were planned, but the zones held the body 3 mm deeper and 10 mm longer at each end, and a support that grips the edge needs no hole. The support is designed later, around real strips ([#9](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/9), [#21](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/21)) |
| Body | As compact as the parts and routing allow: 25.5 deep from the panel edge today, and it grows if routing needs room, up to the 30 cap. 104 long: the ways' 80.16 plus a 12 mm ear past each end way, along the panel's side. The way 6 ear is chamfered at 45° from 15.6 out at the board end to the body's corner. Sensor band 1.5–9 out. One row of output nuts on 13.36 pitch, centres 14 out. There are no holes between the nuts. As a soft target, the parts face keeps a lane about 2 mm wide free of parts from midway between each pair of nuts out to the outboard edge, so a printed divider could slide on from the edge later. Placement and routing drop the lane wherever it gets in the way, without asking. The way 1 ear has the ESP32-C3-MINI-1 lying along the strip at the bottom (y 1.25–14.45), antenna past the board end. Above it is a row of three caps for the pins that face up (EN, IO3 and 3V3), then the multiplexer, then GPIO2's pull-up at the outboard edge. Beside the module, facing way 1, are the two GPIO test pads and the 10 nF for mux 2's common, with the LED's resistor at the top of the corridor; beside the multiplexer are the GPIO8 and GPIO9 pull-ups, the LED, the 10 µF, the multiplexer's 100 nF and the EN pull-up. The way 6 ear has the ground nut, low down (centre a pitch past way 6, 5.25 out), and the second multiplexer above it, lying along the strip (courtyard x −49.0 to −38.4, y 10.2–18.1); the chamfer was cut 2 mm smaller to make room for it ([#29](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/29)). The band behind the nuts holds everything else: the programming footprint over way 1, the programming diode and the supply test pads over way 2, then the supply in a line over ways 4–6. Coordinates: x from the way 1 end, y outboard from the panel edge | The 40 mm to the cable trunking is a CAD figure from hand measurements, so the body is capped at 30 mm until the fit check ([#13](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/13)) firms it up. A smaller body costs less and fits more panels, but no depth below the cap is a target in itself. The SOIC multiplexer is too wide to sit between two nuts, so it goes in the way 1 ear. The band's depth is then set by the inductor between two nuts, over way 6. Five 1.7 × 8 mm divider slots were dropped for #29: with the 1.5 mm keepout for their clip barbs, they left room for only one trace on each side of each nut, and squeezed the corridor behind the nuts to under 1 mm. The divider they served may never be made, and one that grips the edge needs no hole. The ears sit low so they hug the panel. The ground nut's centre is about 16 from way 6's, further than two ways' rings. The antenna reaches 24.0 past way 1's centre, 5.4 past the board end (CAD, [#13](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/13)). On the row where way 1 meets the panel's negative end, the antenna is about 9.5 mm from the nearest negative bus screw, against the 15 aimed for, and under the ground wires; both are accepted. On the other row, where the strip is turned 180°, it meets the positive end instead, 8.2 from way 1's screw. One row keeps the body 8 mm shallower than staggered rows. A single ring touches its neighbour only after swinging 39°, or 50° when the whole bundle swings, so barriers are optional. Check RSSI on the first boards ([#15](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/15), [#21](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/21)) |
| Sensor | Cross Chip CC6905S8-3FB030 (C41421490), $0.56: 3.3 V, ±30 A, 44 mV/A, 0.6 mΩ max (0.45 W at 30 A) | Cheapest stocked 30 A part, and it runs from 3.3 V, so no 5 V rail or dividers. It has no factory temperature compensation; see Accuracy ([sensors](research/way-monitor-current-sensor.md)) |
| ADC | ESP32-C3-MINI-1-H4X (C41349510, extended), $2.65 at 10+ on 2026-10-09, plus two CD4051B 8:1 multiplexers (C21379, preferred part, no fee), one in each ear. Each reads its own end's three ways, current and voltage; the way 1 one also reads the temperature sensor. The H4X is chip revision v1.1, so firmware needs ESP-IDF v5.2 or later (or v5.1.3, v5.0.5, v4.4.7 on older branches) | The C3 has only 4 clean ADC1 channels with Wi-Fi on. A second CD4051B costs no fee, where a 16:1 74HC4067 (C179326) is extended; splitting by end also keeps ways 4–6's signals off the crowded way 1 column, leaving only the selects and one common running the strip. The ESP32-C2 would save about $0.70 a strip, but its stock is thin and its ESPHome support weaker ([ADC](research/way-monitor-adc.md)). The H4X replaces the N4 (C2838502), which is rated to 85 °C and marked NRND; a fully loaded strip can take the module past 85 °C. The H4X is rated to 105 °C, with the same footprint, pins, strapping and in-package flash ([#18](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/18)) |
| Output terminal | Sinhoo SMT M4 nut (C2916378), $0.17. The ring clamps onto a copper pad. Its land is Ø9.37 round a Ø6.4 hole | Assembled with everything else. The current flows ring to pad, so the nut's missing current rating barely matters. Torque about 1.0–1.2 N·m. Sinhoo gives no land; Ø9.37 is PEM's minimum for its SMTSO-M4 ([sheet](https://damlite.pemnet.com/SMTSO-M4-3ET_pdf_1.pdf), checked 2026-10-10), which this nut copies: the same Ø8.74 ±0.13 flange and Ø6.22 pilot. Lever terminals cost $2+ and would mean re-terminating ([terminals](research/way-monitor-terminals-and-copper.md)) |
| Copper | Solid pours on both layers along each way's roughly 20 mm path, masked, with no thermal relief, in `board/lib/copper.tsx`. Line: the finger, 8.9 wide on both faces (the finger less 0.3 a side), up to IP− on the parts face, 7.5 wide there. The ring face's line copper stops 0.3 short of the panel edge. Load: IP+ to the nut, 5 wide on the parts face between the diode cathode and the sensor's caps. On the ring face it starts 6.3 out, just below the IP+ vias, and is 12.4 wide below y 10, 11 above it and a Ø13 disc round the nut, so a ring's tongue lies on its own way's copper. Way 1's stops at x 38.9 below y 11.9, 0.7 short, for the lines from the channel to mux 1. Stitched with 0.3 mm tented vias: 10 at the clamp above the washer, 8 above IP+ and 5 below the nut. Bare copper (HASL) only where the panel clamps: on the parts face from 3.0 mm into the panel to the tip, and the Ø8.13 washer ring on the ring face. GND on the ring face is a bus 0.7 wide along the panel edge, both ears and the band behind the nuts (from 20.8 out). On the parts face it covers the way 1 ear, the band behind the nuts from 19.6 out, and the way 6 ear above mux 2. The way 1 ear is joined to the bus by three vias under the module beside its panel-edge pads; two more above its ground pad tie in the ring face inside the select lines. The module's ground pad has no vias: tscircuit reads a via in it as a short to the pad's paste windows. Way 6's ring face copper is cut back by 1.1 to 1.9 mm on the ground nut's side, below 14.8 out and outside its straight-out ring keepout, for the lanes that turn outboard there. Way copper stays at least 0.5 from the ESP32's GND. The ring face from 1.0 to 6.3 out is the channel: it carries the signal lines the length of the strip (see Routing). 1 oz. No bus current on the board | IPC-2152 widths for a long 30 A trace don't apply to a 20 mm path. The finger can't take the 9.5 mm asked for in [#17](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/17), because 9.5 is the finger's own width. Below IP− only the parts face carries the line, for about 4 mm, which adds about 0.1 W at 30 A. IP− gets no stitching, because the bus runs under it. The nut's plated barrel joins the two faces, so 5 vias round it are enough where the traces beside it leave no room for 8. Pours clear other nets by 0.3 ([#19](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/19)). No load current flows on the ring face below the IP+ vias, so that copper was given to the channel ([#20](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/20)) |
| Power | Every way feeds the supply through its own SS210 diode (C14996, basic), tapped before its sensor. Then a 1 A SMD fuse (Littelfuse 0466001, C151135), an SMBJ33A TVS (C19077586, preferred) and a TI LMR51610X buck (C20539658), 65 V rated. Ground is a wire from an M4 SMT nut to the negative bus. No jumper | The strip runs while any of its ways has a live fuse, works on either side of the panel, and its own draw stays out of every way's reading. The diodes also block reverse polarity. JLC has no basic or preferred fuse or PTC, so the fuse's $3 fee is unavoidable. The XL1509 was dropped because no TVS stays off at 29.2 V (24 V LiFePO4) and also clamps below its 45 V limit. For the same two fees, the LMR51610X costs about $0.80 more and is about 13 points more efficient ([#6](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/6), [power](research/way-monitor-power-supply.md); its cable-supply section is superseded) |
| Pin map and programming | Two CD4051Bs share selects on GPIO5–7, INH to GND, each with 100 nF. Mux 1 (way 1 ear) has its common on GPIO3 and reads S1–S3, V1–V3 and the temperature sensor, with one spare. Its channels are in the order the lines arrive (X2 S1, X4 V1, X1 S2, X6 V2, X7 S3, X0 V3, X3 temperature, X5 spare), so none cross; the schematic is the reference for firmware. Mux 2 (way 6 ear) has its common on GPIO4 and reads S4–S6 and V4–V6, with two spare: S4 on X3, V4 on X0, S5 on X1, V5 on X6, S6 on X4 and V6 on X2, the order their lines leave the channel. Each common has 10 nF at the ESP32 pin; mux 2's runs about 85 mm on the ring face, away from L1 and the switching node. Vn is a 100 kΩ / 6.8 kΩ divider from way n's line copper beside its SS210 anode, before the sensor, so its current stays out of the reading; it reads each way's voltage, shows a blown or pulled fuse, and gives the supply voltage without the diode drop, so there is no separate supply divider ([#29](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/29)). Status LED on GPIO1, with a Ø1.0 test pad on its line beside the module pin; GPIO0 has a test pad of its own. GPIO10 is unused: the LED left it so that the 10 nF at GPIO4 no longer has to straddle its line, hard against the module ([#20](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/20)). Flashed with an Espressif ESP-PROG through J1: six Ø1.5 round SMT pads on a 2×3 2.54 mm grid, on the parts face only, behind way 1's nut at 21.6 out. They are numbered like the ESP-PROG header seen from the parts face, with pin 1 marked in the parts-face silk, and reached by a 2.54 mm double-row 6P pogo clamp slid on from the board edge, so nothing sits in J1's column between its pads and the edge. Its VDD (ESP-PROG on 5 V) feeds the supply node through an SS210. No USB-C, no buttons. Flash off the panel, or with the ground wire fitted; update over Wi-Fi on the panel | Wi-Fi updates make a wired port a first-flash and recovery tool, so it shouldn't cost a part fee. Every USB-C socket and pin header at JLC is extended. The ESP-PROG's auto-reset makes BOOT and RESET buttons unnecessary. A product would buy pre-flashed modules instead. J1 started as a through-hole lock footprint, but its pads were then bare on the ring face under way 1's ring keepout; nowhere on the body is clear of every keepout ([#7](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/7), [ESP32-C3 research](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/4), [#26](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/26)) |
| Ring keepout | Sized for an M4 ring for 4–6 mm² cable at most: a Ø10 ring with a 10 mm wide tongue running outboard past the board edge. Inside a way's ring keepout the ring face carries only that way's own copper; every other net, GND included, crosses on the parts face, and the ring-face GND in the way 1 ear is cut back to it. The straight-out keepout is the minimum. The goal is the keepout swung ±20° about the nut, and ±39° where it costs nothing | A 30 A way never needs more than 4 mm² cable, and most ways use 1.5–2.5 mm² with smaller rings. ±39° stacks the largest ring on the largest swing, and it cost J1 its through-hole pads and the ring face between the nuts. Mask isn't insulation: a ring that turns while its nut is torqued can scrape it, and a way's 12–24 V landing on GND is a dead short through the fuse. 39° is where a ring meets its neighbour, and that contact only joins two fused ways, not a short to GND ([#26](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/26)) |
| Accuracy | One-off zero and gain calibration per way. Automatic re-zeroing when a way sits steady near zero, and always when its voltage reads 0 V, since then no current can flow. One temperature sensor per strip on a multiplexer channel: a diode-connected MMBT3904 (C20526, basic, $0.0115) biased at about 265 µA by a 10 kΩ from 3.3 V, read as Vbe (about 0.65 V, −2 mV/°C). The firmware records Vbe whenever it zeroes a way, and learns each way's offset tempco from its re-zeros (a hot-and-cold bench zero is optional). Only the change in temperature since the way was zeroed is used, so the sensor needs no calibration of its own. Its absolute reading (±5–10 °C) is good enough for an over-temperature alert. The ESP32-C3's internal sensor is the backup. Place the sensor on the parts face, in the sensor band between ways 3 and 4, on the GND pour, away from the buck and the ESP32. It reads board temperature, not each CC6905's die, so self-heating at high current stays uncorrected | The CC6905's offset can drift up to ±0.45 A over its full temperature range. The datasheet gives a spread, not a curve, so each way needs its own tempco. JLC has no basic or preferred NTC; the transistor saves one extended part type, about $3 an order ([#16](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/16)) |
| Ordering | One strip design: order 5 bare boards, have 4 assembled. Economic PCBA. Black mask, white silk, lead-free HASL | Identical strips avoid the multi-design surcharge a mixed panel would carry ([JLC](research/jlc-panelised-assembly.md)). Only black or white mask was wanted. Economic PCBA offers ENIG on green only, and black ENIG needs Standard PCBA, about $45–61 more per order plus a panel. The ring terminals are tin-plated, so tin pads make an ordinary bolted joint ([#24](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/24)) |
| Silkscreen | Ring face, all white silk, no art in the finish. The banner's BLADE READER outline, 58 × 4.6 mm, behind the nuts. A framed vertical ブレードリーダー sign in the way 1 ear. Three chevrons per column, finger to nut, with way 1's filled as the Sensor 1 mark. A spec plate between the columns: revision and date, derating, output nut torque (M4 1.0–1.2 Nm), licence, and the repo path XDGFX/BLADE-READER without the host. The banner quote, two lines per finger. GND with an earth symbol, and DESIGNED IN AUSTRALIA with the Southern Cross along the chamfer. Render and source: [`prototype/silkscreen-concepts`](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/tree/prototype/silkscreen-concepts/board/prototype-silkscreen). Parts face, echoing the ring face: the wordmark at 17 mm between F1 and the test pads, the JLC order-number marker in a frame, two chevrons per finger with way 1's filled, S1–S6 over the sensors, and designators only on ICs and diodes. Pin-1 dots, cathode bars, and Gaff's origami unicorn in the way 6 ear. LED1 has a tick beside its cathode pad, on the side away from the anode, and no designator: nothing else fits beside it. No stock footprint silk. Render and source: [`prototype/parts-face-silkscreen`](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/tree/prototype/parts-face-silkscreen/board/prototype-parts-silkscreen) | The board should be beautiful, not only labelled ([#22](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/22)). The parts face is seen at unboxing and in photos, and is against the panel once fitted, so it carries the ring face's look rather than full designators ([#28](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/28)). The derating and torque sit in the sensor band because it stays visible with the rings and cables fitted. Mono text avoids the stroke font's missing `·°:%`, except the drawn ° ([#23](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/23), [#24](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/24)) |
| Solder paste | Written by `board/scripts/paste.ts`, which `fab` runs after the silk pass; `bun run paste` runs it without routing. Only a part JLC places is pasted, and only on the parts face. Each aperture is its pad drawn 0.025 mm inside every edge, 82 % of the pad or more. Each nut gets four arcs of a ring on its land, from Ø7.0 to Ø9.0 with 0.6 mm gaps, 0.3 mm clear of the Ø6.4 hole. Nothing on the way-screw holes, J1's pads, the test pads or the ring face. The pass also redraws U3's pads as plain rectangles and writes the paste as outlines, so no Gerber uses the `%LR%` rotation command, and it fails if paste is on the ring face, over a hole, off its pad, opens under 80 % of it, or on a part that isn't placed. `F_Fab.gbr` is left out of the zip. JLC reworks the apertures of ICs and of 0805 and larger parts to its own standard unless the order remarks ask it not to: let it, since it knows its stencil and process, and it adds no paste where the layer has none. The nut's land is Ø9.3, 0.07 under PEM's minimum of Ø9.37 for its own SMTSO-M4 | tscircuit's default is 70 % of each pad each way, 49 % of the area, and a full disc over every plated hole on both faces. IPC-7525 as quoted takes 0.03–0.08 mm off a leaded part's aperture in width and length, and TI's stencil for the LMR51610 is 1:1 at 0.125 mm ([datasheet](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/lmr51610.pdf)); 0.025 a side is the low end of that, and keeps the smallest aperture's area ratio above 0.66 at any stencil up to 0.18 mm (0.60 at 0.2). Espressif, CrossChip and Sinhoo give no stencil; Espressif says only not to over-paste the module's ground pad, which is already nine windows covering 65 % of its area. PEM shows segmented rings with no paste over the hole and tested a two-spoke pattern at 0.17 mm ([SMTSO-M4](https://damlite.pemnet.com/SMTSO-M4-3ET_pdf_1.pdf)); four arcs give more paste for a thinner stencil. A pasted pad carries flux residue that a pogo pin or probe has to break through, so J1 and the test pads stay bare HASL. Some CAM tools ignore `%LR%`, which would turn U3's pads sideways at 0.95 mm pitch, and JLC says nothing either way. JLC's stencil rules: [order instructions](https://jlcpcb.com/help/article/instructions-for-stencil-order), [opening standard](https://jlcpcb.com/help/article/opening-process-standard-of-stencil), all checked 2026-10-10 ([#42](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/42)) |
| Placement | Each way's column is the same. The CC6905 sits in the sensor band at Y 4 with its IP pins towards −X, IP− towards the finger, so current runs straight up the column: finger, IP−, IP+, nut. The SS210 stands beside IP− with its anode down on the line copper. The sensor's three caps sit in a row above its signal pins. Ways 1–3 shift this column 0.5 mm towards the way 1 end and ways 4–6 towards the way 6 end, which makes room for the MMBT3904 between ways 3 and 4. In the body, the ESP32, its decoupling and EN parts, the status LED, the strapping resistors, the 10 µF, the two GPIO test pads and the multiplexer with its decoupling are in the way 1 ear: each small part stands where its line already runs, so the corridor between way 1's column and the module is left to the lines for J1 ([#33](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/33)). Only the caps that must sit at their pins are hard against the module: the 3V3, EN and IO3 caps in the row above it and the 10 nF beside it. GPIO2's pull-up, the LED and the LED's resistor stand at least 1 mm clear ([#38](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/38)). Behind the nuts are: the programming footprint over way 1; the programming diode and the supply test pads over way 2; then, from way 4 towards way 6, the fuse, TVS diode, input caps, buck, inductor and output caps. Plan: [`placement-rev-a.png`](assets/placement-rev-a.png). The PCB X axis is seen from the parts face, so way 1 is at +X | VSUP leaves up the diode's side and VOUT up the signal side. With the divider slots gone, the gap between two nut pads is at least 3.46 mm usable on the parts face, about 12 traces, and parts can sit in it. The ring face there is inside a neighbour's swung ring keepout above about y 11, so nothing else crosses on it. Everything between the ends runs along the band behind the nuts, between and around the parts there. Each way's divider sits in the sensor band beside its SS210, outboard of the line copper, with a via from its 6.8 kΩ to the ring-face GND bus. The MMBT3904 stands in the gap between ways 3 and 4, 7.8 out, above the channel, with its bias resistor above it; its GND joins way 4's cap GND. It moved up from beside way 3's diode so that way 3's divider could stand there like the others and reach the channel ([#30](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/30), [#20](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/20)).
