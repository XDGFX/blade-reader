# Design basis

The decisions taken so far, with enough of the reasoning and numbers to judge them. The detail
and sources are in [`research/`](research/). The decisions were made in the private Hailey repo,
whose issue numbers appear in the research notes as `XDGFX/hailey#NNN`.

Prices are USD from JLCPCB/LCSC on 2026-10-09, at 10–50 quantity.

## Requirements

- Measure the current on every way of a 12-way blade fuse panel. Every way gets a sensor,
  spare ways included.
- About ±0.3 A is good enough. Very small loads reading as zero is accepted.
- Don't limit the panel: 30 A per way, 100 A per panel.
- Existing #8 / M4 ring terminals must fit without re-crimping. The panel cover must still fit.
- Report to Home Assistant over Wi-Fi via ESPHome.
- Accept 12 V and 24 V systems.
- Cheap, and fully assembled by JLCPCB, with no hand soldering.

## Decisions

| Area | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Topology | Identical, standalone 6-way strips, two per 12-way panel, each with its own ESP32 | Cables between strips cost about the same as three more ESPs. Standalone strips also avoid a ground-drop offset and a single point of failure. Accepted costs: about 1 W more standby draw, and one HA device per strip |
| Mechanical | A comb. Each finger, 9.5 mm wide, slides sideways into a way's pocket at screw-pad height and is clamped under the panel's own #8 screw, through a 5.4 mm round hole. The strip sits outside the panel edge and needs 29 mm of space beside it. Parts face down, ring face up, so rings are screwed on from above. Outline: [comb geometry](assets/comb-outline-rev-a.svg) | Barriers stand 4–8.5 mm above the screw pads, so a flat board can't lie under all six screws. A 1.6 mm finger leaves about 4 mm of thread engaged ([dimensions](research/fuse-panel-dimensions.md)). 9.5 mm and 5.4 mm also fit the Blue Sea clones. A hole rather than an open slot, because the old rings have to come off the screws anyway; the hole keeps the tip solid and the strip captive ([#8](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/8)) |
| Fixing | The six way screws are the only required fixing. The body has no fixing holes. An optional 3D-printed support, screwed to the bulkhead, can slide over the edge of the board to carry the body. Install notes: fit the strip and torque all six way screws first, then fit the output rings; tie output cables to the bulkhead within about 100 mm | Six clamped screws hold the strip in the panel's plane with a wide margin, and the round holes keep it captive while any screw is in. The weak case is the body bending. Pushing down to drive an output bolt or pulling a cable puts about 2–4 N·m on the finger roots, roughly 80–160 MPa against FR4's roughly 400 MPa. No SMT standoff comes in the 14.3 mm needed, and they are extended parts (about $3.30–3.60). M3 holes with 12 × 12 mm keep-clear zones were planned, but the zones held the body 3 mm deeper and 10 mm longer at each end, and a support that grips the edge needs no hole. The support is designed later, around real strips ([#9](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/9), [#21](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/21)) |
| Body | 29 deep from the panel edge. 100.16 long: the ways' 80.16 plus a 10 mm extension past each end way, along the panel's side. Sensor band 1.5–9 out. One row of output nuts on 13.36 pitch, centres 14 out. Between the nuts are five 1.7 × 10 mm NPTH slots, y 10–20, so a printed divider can clip in later. The parts face is kept clear of parts for 1.5 mm round each slot, for the clip's barbs. The ESP32-C3-MINI-1 lies along the strip in the way 1 extension's outboard corner (y 14.4–27.6), antenna past the board end. Its pad area reaches 1.2 mm over way 1's column, above the nut. 3V3, EN, IO2 and IO3 face the outboard edge, with their 0402s in a row between the module and the edge; the programming footprint, the LED and the other strapping parts are under the module. The multiplexer sits behind the slots over way 3 (y 21–28.5). The way 6 extension has the ground nut (centre 5 from the board end, 6.4 out) with the supply above it; the fuse and TVS diode sit behind the slots over way 6. Test pads sit one behind each nut. Coordinates: x from the way 1 end, y outboard from the panel edge | The 40 mm to the cable trunking is a CAD figure from hand measurements, so the body is kept to 30 mm at most. Ends of about 10 mm stay clear of the wires past the strip; the antenna reaches 15.4 past the end way. A 10 mm end can't hold both the multiplexer and the module, so the multiplexer moves behind the slots, and the mux's height there sets the depth. That is also why the slots are 10 long, not 11: they still cover a ring on a nut at 14. On a 12-way panel one strip's ESP32 sits under the ground wires, which is accepted. One row keeps the body 8 mm shallower than staggered rows. A single ring touches its neighbour only after swinging 39°, or 50° when the whole bundle swings, so barriers are optional. The slots also separate the ways' copper. Check RSSI on the first boards ([#15](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/15), [#21](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/21)) |
| Sensor | Cross Chip CC6905S8-3FB030 (C41421490), $0.56: 3.3 V, ±30 A, 44 mV/A, 0.6 mΩ max (0.45 W at 30 A) | Cheapest stocked 30 A part, and it runs from 3.3 V, so no 5 V rail or dividers. It has no factory temperature compensation; see Accuracy ([sensors](research/way-monitor-current-sensor.md)) |
| ADC | ESP32-C3-MINI-1-H4X (C41349510, extended), $2.65 at 10+ on 2026-10-09, plus one CD4051B 8:1 multiplexer (C21379, preferred part, no fee). Six ways, the temperature sensor and the supply divider. The H4X is chip revision v1.1, so firmware needs ESP-IDF v5.2 or later (or v5.1.3, v5.0.5, v4.4.7 on older branches) | The C3 has only 4 clean ADC1 channels with Wi-Fi on. The ESP32-C2 would save about $0.70 a strip, but its stock is thin and its ESPHome support weaker ([ADC](research/way-monitor-adc.md)). The H4X replaces the N4 (C2838502), which is rated to 85 °C and marked NRND; a fully loaded strip can take the module past 85 °C. The H4X is rated to 105 °C, with the same footprint, pins, strapping and in-package flash ([#18](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/18)) |
| Output terminal | Sinhoo SMT M4 nut (C2916378), $0.17. The ring clamps onto a copper pad | Assembled with everything else. The current flows ring to pad, so the nut's missing current rating barely matters. Torque about 1.0–1.2 N·m. Lever terminals cost $2+ and would mean re-terminating ([terminals](research/way-monitor-terminals-and-copper.md)) |
| Copper | Full-width pours on both layers, stitched with vias, along each way's roughly 20 mm path. 1 oz is fine. No bus current on the board | IPC-2152 widths for a long 30 A trace don't apply to a 20 mm path |
| Power | Every way feeds the supply through its own SS210 diode (C14996, basic), tapped before its sensor. Then a 1 A SMD fuse (Littelfuse 0466001, C151135), an SMBJ33A TVS (C19077586, preferred) and a TI LMR51610X buck (C20539658), 65 V rated. Ground is a wire from an M4 SMT nut to the negative bus. No jumper | The strip runs while any of its ways has a live fuse, works on either side of the panel, and its own draw stays out of every way's reading. The diodes also block reverse polarity. JLC has no basic or preferred fuse or PTC, so the fuse's $3 fee is unavoidable. The XL1509 was dropped because no TVS stays off at 29.2 V (24 V LiFePO4) and also clamps below its 45 V limit. For the same two fees, the LMR51610X costs about $0.80 more and is about 13 points more efficient ([#6](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/6), [power](research/way-monitor-power-supply.md); its cable-supply section is superseded) |
| Pin map and programming | CD4051B common on GPIO3 with 10 nF at the pin, selects on GPIO5–7, INH to GND. Mux inputs: six ways, the temperature sensor, and a 100 kΩ / 6.8 kΩ divider from the supply node. Status LED on GPIO10. GPIO0, 1 and 4 to test pads. Flashed with an Espressif ESP-PROG through an empty 2×3 2.54 mm footprint with offset "lock" holes: a header is pushed in, not soldered. Its VDD (ESP-PROG on 5 V) feeds the supply node through an SS210. No USB-C, no buttons. Flash off the panel, or with the ground wire fitted; update over Wi-Fi on the panel | Wi-Fi updates make a wired port a first-flash and recovery tool, so it shouldn't cost a part fee. Every USB-C socket and pin header at JLC is extended. The ESP-PROG's auto-reset makes BOOT and RESET buttons unnecessary. A product would buy pre-flashed modules instead ([#7](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/7), [ESP32-C3 research](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/4)) |
| Accuracy | One-off zero and gain calibration per way. Automatic re-zeroing when a way sits steady near zero. One temperature sensor per strip on a multiplexer channel: a diode-connected MMBT3904 (C20526, basic, $0.0115) biased at about 265 µA by a 10 kΩ from 3.3 V, read as Vbe (about 0.65 V, −2 mV/°C). The firmware records Vbe whenever it zeroes a way, and learns each way's offset tempco from its re-zeros (a hot-and-cold bench zero is optional). Only the change in temperature since the way was zeroed is used, so the sensor needs no calibration of its own. Its absolute reading (±5–10 °C) is good enough for an over-temperature alert. The ESP32-C3's internal sensor is the backup. Place the sensor on the parts face, in the sensor band between ways 3 and 4, on the GND pour, away from the buck and the ESP32. It reads board temperature, not each CC6905's die, so self-heating at high current stays uncorrected | The CC6905's offset can drift up to ±0.45 A over its full temperature range. The datasheet gives a spread, not a curve, so each way needs its own tempco. JLC has no basic or preferred NTC; the transistor saves one extended part type, about $3 an order ([#16](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/16)) |
| Ordering | One strip design: order 5 bare boards, have 4 assembled. Economic PCBA | Identical strips avoid the multi-design surcharge a mixed panel would carry ([JLC](research/jlc-panelised-assembly.md)) |
| Placement | Each way's column is the same. The CC6905 sits in the sensor band at Y 4 with its IP pins towards −X, IP− towards the finger, so current runs straight up the column: finger, IP−, IP+, nut. The SS210 stands beside IP− with its anode down on the line copper. The sensor's three caps sit in a row above its signal pins. Ways 1–3 shift this column 0.5 mm towards the way 1 end and ways 4–6 towards the way 6 end, which makes room for the MMBT3904 between ways 3 and 4. In the body, the ESP32, its decoupling, EN and strapping parts, status LED and programming footprint are in the way 1 extension. The multiplexer, its decoupling, the supply divider and the temperature sensor's bias resistor are behind the slots, over way 3 and the slot between ways 3 and 4. The buck with its inductor and capacitors, and the ground nut, are in the way 6 extension, with the fuse and TVS diode behind the slots over way 6. Plan: [`placement-rev-a.png`](assets/placement-rev-a.png). The PCB X axis is seen from the parts face, so way 1 is at +X | Each column has only two copper paths past its nut: between the nut pad and the slot on each side there's room for one trace. VSUP leaves up the diode's side and VOUT up the signal side. GND, 3V3 and TEMP can't pass the nut row in the columns, so they need another way across. Everything between the ends runs along the 9 mm band behind the slots; past the multiplexer, only on the ring face. IO lines leave the module's inboard end over way 1's nut, and only a few lines can pass between that nut and the module to the parts under it ([#21](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/21)). The rest is for [pours](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/19) and [routing](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/20) ([#12](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/12)) |
| Tooling | tscircuit 0.0.2786, pinned exactly in `board/package.json`. `board/bun.lock` is the real pin, because the CLI it pulls in floats. Run everything with `bun run` in `board/`: `build` is the DRC gate, `fab` builds and then writes Gerbers, BOM and CPL to `board/fab/`, `placement` checks placement and renders the board without routing, and `step` writes the board solid for Fusion, also without routing. The STEP has the ESP32, mux, supply and passives, but not the CC6905, SS210 or nut models, which fail to merge. The pinned version doesn't rotate a footprint's courtyard rect with the part, so a rotated passive or footprinter-string part can show false courtyard overlaps or hide real ones. The SS210 has a hand-written footprint with an outline courtyard for this reason; check pad gaps on the rest. Don't use the global `tsci`, which runs the global CLI, or `tsci export` alone, which skips the DRC. Use polygon pours for current paths (its autorouter narrows wide traces), keep copper out of the ESP32's antenna area, and check every rotation in JLC's assembly preview | Shown to work in a trial run with these exact parts ([#5](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/5)). The scaffold builds a placeholder board end to end ([#10](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/10)) |

## Open

- Board rules: JLC trace and via minimums, the antenna keepout, edge clearance.
- Generic blade fuse box compatibility: their terminal pitch and style.
- A design-rule check, a review of JLC's assembly preview, and a final costing before ordering.
- ESPHome configuration, once a board exists.
