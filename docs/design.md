# Design basis

The decisions taken so far, with enough of the reasoning and numbers to judge them. The detail
and sources are in [`research/`](research/). The decisions were made in the private Hailey repo,
whose issue numbers appear in the research notes as `XDGFX/hailey#NNN`.

Prices are USD from JLCPCB/LCSC on 2026-10-09, at 10–50 quantity.

## Requirements

- Measure the current on every way of a 12-way blade fuse panel. Every way gets a sensor,
  spare ways included.
- Read every way's voltage too, so a blown or pulled fuse can raise an alert.
- About ±0.3 A is good enough. Very small loads reading as zero is accepted.
- Don't limit the panel: 30 A per way, 100 A per panel.
- Existing #8 / M4 ring terminals must fit without re-crimping. The panel cover must still fit.
- Report to Home Assistant over Wi-Fi via ESPHome.
- Accept 12 V and 24 V systems.
- Cheap, and fully assembled by JLCPCB, with no hand soldering.

## Decisions

| Area | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Topology | Identical, standalone 6-way strips, two per 12-way panel, each with its own ESP32 | Cables between strips cost about the same as three more ESPs. Standalone strips also avoid a ground-drop offset and a single point of failure. Accepted costs: about 1 W more standby draw, and one HA device per strip |
| Mechanical | A comb. Each finger, 9.5 mm wide, slides sideways into a way's pocket at screw-pad height and is clamped under the panel's own #8 screw, through a 5.4 mm round hole. The strip sits outside the panel edge and needs 25.5 mm of space beside it. Parts face down, ring face up, so rings are screwed on from above. Outline: [comb geometry](assets/comb-outline-rev-a.svg) | Barriers stand 4–8.5 mm above the screw pads, so a flat board can't lie under all six screws. A 1.6 mm finger leaves about 4 mm of thread engaged ([dimensions](research/fuse-panel-dimensions.md)). 9.5 mm and 5.4 mm also fit the Blue Sea clones. A hole rather than an open slot, because the old rings have to come off the screws anyway; the hole keeps the tip solid and the strip captive ([#8](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/8)) |
| Fixing | The six way screws are the only required fixing. The body has no fixing holes. An optional 3D-printed support, screwed to the bulkhead, can slide over the edge of the board to carry the body. Install notes: fit the strip and torque all six way screws first, keeping the panel's own lock washer under each, then fit the output rings with an M4 split spring washer under each bolt (Bunnings: Pinnacle M4 stainless spring washer, 12 pack, item 0851291, AUD 2.63 on 2026-10-10; 4 mm bore, 8 mm OD, so it sits inside the ring); tie output cables to the bulkhead within about 100 mm. Re-check every torque after the first warm run. On the first boards, check the torque after a hot soak at load | Both joints clamp FR4, which creeps under load when warm, so the bolts lose preload over heat cycles, and a loose 30 A joint heats itself further. A spring washer keeps the clamp up while the board settles. A conical (DIN 6796) washer would hold it better, but neither Bunnings nor Jaycar stocks one, and at 9 mm OD the M4 size only just fits the ring; the panel's CAD already shows a lock washer on every screw ([dimensions](research/fuse-panel-dimensions.md)). Six clamped screws hold the strip in the panel's plane with a wide margin, and the round holes keep it captive while any screw is in. The weak case is the body bending. Pushing down to drive an output bolt or pulling a cable puts about 2–4 N·m on the finger roots, roughly 80–160 MPa against FR4's roughly 400 MPa. No SMT standoff comes in the 14.3 mm needed, and they are extended parts (about $3.30–3.60). M3 holes with 12 × 12 mm keep-clear zones were planned, but the zones held the body 3 mm deeper and 10 mm longer at each end, and a support that grips the edge needs no hole. The support is designed later, around real strips ([#9](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/9), [#21](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/21)) |
| Body | As compact as the parts and routing allow: 25.5 deep from the panel edge today, and it grows if routing needs room, up to the 30 cap. 104 long: the ways' 80.16 plus a 12 mm ear past each end way, along the panel's side. The way 6 ear is chamfered at 45° from 15.6 out at the board end to the body's corner. Sensor band 1.5–9 out. One row of output nuts on 13.36 pitch, centres 14 out. There are no holes between the nuts. As a soft target, the parts face keeps a lane about 2 mm wide free of parts from midway between each pair of nuts out to the outboard edge, so a printed divider could slide on from the edge later. Placement and routing drop the lane wherever it gets in the way, without asking. The way 1 ear has the ESP32-C3-MINI-1 lying along the strip at the bottom (y 1.25–14.45), antenna past the board end. Above it are the 0402s for 3V3, EN, IO2 and IO3, which face up, then the multiplexer. The way 6 ear has the ground nut, low down (centre a pitch past way 6, 5.25 out), and the second multiplexer above it, lying along the strip (courtyard x −49.0 to −38.4, y 10.2–18.1); the chamfer was cut 2 mm smaller to make room for it ([#29](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/29)). The band behind the nuts holds everything else: the programming footprint, LED and strapping resistors over way 1, the programming diode and test pads over way 2, the temperature bias over way 3, then the supply in a line over ways 4–6. Coordinates: x from the way 1 end, y outboard from the panel edge | The 40 mm to the cable trunking is a CAD figure from hand measurements, so the body is capped at 30 mm until the fit check ([#13](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/13)) firms it up. A smaller body costs less and fits more panels, but no depth below the cap is a target in itself. The SOIC multiplexer is too wide to sit between two nuts, so it goes in the way 1 ear. The band's depth is then set by the inductor between two nuts, over way 6. Five 1.7 × 8 mm divider slots were dropped for #29: with the 1.5 mm keepout for their clip barbs, they left room for only one trace on each side of each nut, and squeezed the corridor behind the nuts to under 1 mm. The divider they served may never be made, and one that grips the edge needs no hole. The ears sit low so they hug the panel. The ground nut's centre is about 16 from way 6's, further than two ways' rings. The antenna reaches 24.0 past way 1's centre, 5.4 past the board end (CAD, [#13](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/13)). On the row where way 1 meets the panel's negative end, the antenna is about 9.5 mm from the nearest negative bus screw, against the 15 aimed for, and under the ground wires; both are accepted. On the other row, where the strip is turned 180°, it meets the positive end instead, 8.2 from way 1's screw. One row keeps the body 8 mm shallower than staggered rows. A single ring touches its neighbour only after swinging 39°, or 50° when the whole bundle swings, so barriers are optional. Check RSSI on the first boards ([#15](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/15), [#21](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/21)) |
| Sensor | Cross Chip CC6905S8-3FB030 (C41421490), $0.56: 3.3 V, ±30 A, 44 mV/A, 0.6 mΩ max (0.45 W at 30 A) | Cheapest stocked 30 A part, and it runs from 3.3 V, so no 5 V rail or dividers. It has no factory temperature compensation; see Accuracy ([sensors](research/way-monitor-current-sensor.md)) |
| ADC | ESP32-C3-MINI-1-H4X (C41349510, extended), $2.65 at 10+ on 2026-10-09, plus two CD4051B 8:1 multiplexers (C21379, preferred part, no fee), one in each ear. Each reads its own end's three ways, current and voltage; the way 1 one also reads the temperature sensor. The H4X is chip revision v1.1, so firmware needs ESP-IDF v5.2 or later (or v5.1.3, v5.0.5, v4.4.7 on older branches) | The C3 has only 4 clean ADC1 channels with Wi-Fi on. A second CD4051B costs no fee, where a 16:1 74HC4067 (C179326) is extended; splitting by end also keeps ways 4–6's signals off the crowded way 1 column, leaving only the selects and one common running the strip. The ESP32-C2 would save about $0.70 a strip, but its stock is thin and its ESPHome support weaker ([ADC](research/way-monitor-adc.md)). The H4X replaces the N4 (C2838502), which is rated to 85 °C and marked NRND; a fully loaded strip can take the module past 85 °C. The H4X is rated to 105 °C, with the same footprint, pins, strapping and in-package flash ([#18](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/18)) |
| Output terminal | Sinhoo SMT M4 nut (C2916378), $0.17. The ring clamps onto a copper pad | Assembled with everything else. The current flows ring to pad, so the nut's missing current rating barely matters. Torque about 1.0–1.2 N·m. Lever terminals cost $2+ and would mean re-terminating ([terminals](research/way-monitor-terminals-and-copper.md)) |
| Copper | Solid pours on both layers along each way's roughly 20 mm path, masked, with no thermal relief, in `board/lib/copper.tsx`. Line: the finger, 8.9 wide on both faces (the finger less 0.3 a side), up to IP− on the parts face, 7.5 wide there. The ring face's line copper stops 0.3 short of the panel edge. Load: IP+ to the nut, 5 wide on the parts face between the diode cathode and the sensor's caps. On the ring face it is 12.4 wide below y 10, 11 above it and a Ø13 disc round the nut, so a ring's tongue lies on its own way's copper. Stitched with 0.3 mm tented vias: 10 at the clamp above the washer, 8 above IP+ and 5 below the nut. Bare copper (HASL) only where the panel clamps: on the parts face from 3.0 mm into the panel to the tip, and the Ø8.13 washer ring on the ring face. GND on the ring face is a bus 0.7 wide along the panel edge, both ears and the band behind the nuts (from 20.8 out). On the parts face it covers the way 1 ear and a patch under the MMBT3904. Way copper stays at least 0.5 from the ESP32's GND. The ring face from 1.0 to 2.6 out is left for 3V3 and TEMP to the sensors. 1 oz. No bus current on the board | IPC-2152 widths for a long 30 A trace don't apply to a 20 mm path. The finger can't take the 9.5 mm asked for in [#17](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/17), because 9.5 is the finger's own width. Below IP− only the parts face carries the line, for about 4 mm, which adds about 0.1 W at 30 A. IP− gets no stitching, because the bus runs under it. The nut's plated barrel joins the two faces, so 5 vias round it are enough where the traces beside it leave no room for 8. Pours clear other nets by 0.3 ([#19](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/19)) |
| Power | Every way feeds the supply through its own SS210 diode (C14996, basic), tapped before its sensor. Then a 1 A SMD fuse (Littelfuse 0466001, C151135), an SMBJ33A TVS (C19077586, preferred) and a TI LMR51610X buck (C20539658), 65 V rated. Ground is a wire from an M4 SMT nut to the negative bus. No jumper | The strip runs while any of its ways has a live fuse, works on either side of the panel, and its own draw stays out of every way's reading. The diodes also block reverse polarity. JLC has no basic or preferred fuse or PTC, so the fuse's $3 fee is unavoidable. The XL1509 was dropped because no TVS stays off at 29.2 V (24 V LiFePO4) and also clamps below its 45 V limit. For the same two fees, the LMR51610X costs about $0.80 more and is about 13 points more efficient ([#6](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/6), [power](research/way-monitor-power-supply.md); its cable-supply section is superseded) |
| Pin map and programming | Two CD4051Bs share selects on GPIO5–7, INH to GND, each with 100 nF. Mux 1 (way 1 ear) has its common on GPIO3 and reads S1–S3, V1–V3 and the temperature sensor, with one spare. Mux 2 (way 6 ear) has its common on GPIO4 and reads S4–S6 and V4–V6, with two spare. Each common has 10 nF at the ESP32 pin; mux 2's runs about 85 mm on the ring face, away from L1 and the switching node. Vn is a 100 kΩ / 6.8 kΩ divider from way n's line copper beside its SS210 anode, before the sensor, so its current stays out of the reading; it reads each way's voltage, shows a blown or pulled fuse, and gives the supply voltage without the diode drop, so there is no separate supply divider ([#29](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/29)). Status LED on GPIO10. GPIO0 and 1 to test pads. Flashed with an Espressif ESP-PROG through J1: six Ø1.5 round SMT pads on a 2×3 2.54 mm grid, on the parts face only, behind way 1's nut at 21.6 out. They are numbered like the ESP-PROG header seen from the parts face, with pin 1 marked in the parts-face silk, and reached by a 2.54 mm double-row 6P pogo clamp slid on from the board edge, so nothing sits in J1's column between its pads and the edge. Its VDD (ESP-PROG on 5 V) feeds the supply node through an SS210. No USB-C, no buttons. Flash off the panel, or with the ground wire fitted; update over Wi-Fi on the panel | Wi-Fi updates make a wired port a first-flash and recovery tool, so it shouldn't cost a part fee. Every USB-C socket and pin header at JLC is extended. The ESP-PROG's auto-reset makes BOOT and RESET buttons unnecessary. A product would buy pre-flashed modules instead. J1 started as a through-hole lock footprint, but its pads were then bare on the ring face under way 1's ring keepout; nowhere on the body is clear of every keepout ([#7](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/7), [ESP32-C3 research](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/4), [#26](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/26)) |
| Ring keepout | Sized for an M4 ring for 4–6 mm² cable at most: a Ø10 ring with a 10 mm wide tongue running outboard past the board edge. Inside a way's ring keepout the ring face carries only that way's own copper; every other net, GND included, crosses on the parts face, and the ring-face GND in the way 1 ear is cut back to it. The straight-out keepout is the minimum. The goal is the keepout swung ±20° about the nut, and ±39° where it costs nothing | A 30 A way never needs more than 4 mm² cable, and most ways use 1.5–2.5 mm² with smaller rings. ±39° stacks the largest ring on the largest swing, and it cost J1 its through-hole pads and the ring face between the nuts. Mask isn't insulation: a ring that turns while its nut is torqued can scrape it, and a way's 12–24 V landing on GND is a dead short through the fuse. 39° is where a ring meets its neighbour, and that contact only joins two fused ways, not a short to GND ([#26](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/26)) |
| Accuracy | One-off zero and gain calibration per way. Automatic re-zeroing when a way sits steady near zero, and always when its voltage reads 0 V, since then no current can flow. One temperature sensor per strip on a multiplexer channel: a diode-connected MMBT3904 (C20526, basic, $0.0115) biased at about 265 µA by a 10 kΩ from 3.3 V, read as Vbe (about 0.65 V, −2 mV/°C). The firmware records Vbe whenever it zeroes a way, and learns each way's offset tempco from its re-zeros (a hot-and-cold bench zero is optional). Only the change in temperature since the way was zeroed is used, so the sensor needs no calibration of its own. Its absolute reading (±5–10 °C) is good enough for an over-temperature alert. The ESP32-C3's internal sensor is the backup. Place the sensor on the parts face, in the sensor band between ways 3 and 4, on the GND pour, away from the buck and the ESP32. It reads board temperature, not each CC6905's die, so self-heating at high current stays uncorrected | The CC6905's offset can drift up to ±0.45 A over its full temperature range. The datasheet gives a spread, not a curve, so each way needs its own tempco. JLC has no basic or preferred NTC; the transistor saves one extended part type, about $3 an order ([#16](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/16)) |
| Ordering | One strip design: order 5 bare boards, have 4 assembled. Economic PCBA. Black mask, white silk, lead-free HASL | Identical strips avoid the multi-design surcharge a mixed panel would carry ([JLC](research/jlc-panelised-assembly.md)). Only black or white mask was wanted. Economic PCBA offers ENIG on green only, and black ENIG needs Standard PCBA, about $45–61 more per order plus a panel. The ring terminals are tin-plated, so tin pads make an ordinary bolted joint ([#24](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/24)) |
| Silkscreen | Ring face, all white silk, no art in the finish. The banner's BLADE READER outline, 58 × 4.6 mm, behind the nuts. A framed vertical ブレードリーダー sign in the way 1 ear. Three chevrons per column, finger to nut, with way 1's filled as the Sensor 1 mark. A spec plate between the columns: revision and date, derating, output nut torque (M4 1.0–1.2 Nm), licence, and the repo path XDGFX/BLADE-READER without the host. The banner quote, two lines per finger. GND with an earth symbol, and DESIGNED IN AUSTRALIA with the Southern Cross along the chamfer. Render and source: [`prototype/silkscreen-concepts`](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/tree/prototype/silkscreen-concepts/board/prototype-silkscreen). Parts face, echoing the ring face: the wordmark at 17 mm between F1 and the test pads, the JLC order-number marker in a frame, two chevrons per finger with way 1's filled, S1–S6 over the sensors, and designators only on ICs and diodes. Pin-1 dots, cathode bars, and Gaff's origami unicorn in the way 6 ear. No stock footprint silk. Render and source: [`prototype/parts-face-silkscreen`](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/tree/prototype/parts-face-silkscreen/board/prototype-parts-silkscreen) | The board should be beautiful, not only labelled ([#22](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/22)). The parts face is seen at unboxing and in photos, and is against the panel once fitted, so it carries the ring face's look rather than full designators ([#28](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/28)). The derating and torque sit in the sensor band because it stays visible with the rings and cables fitted. Mono text avoids the stroke font's missing `·°:%`, except the drawn ° ([#23](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/23), [#24](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/24)) |
| Placement | Each way's column is the same. The CC6905 sits in the sensor band at Y 4 with its IP pins towards −X, IP− towards the finger, so current runs straight up the column: finger, IP−, IP+, nut. The SS210 stands beside IP− with its anode down on the line copper. The sensor's three caps sit in a row above its signal pins. Ways 1–3 shift this column 0.5 mm towards the way 1 end and ways 4–6 towards the way 6 end, which makes room for the MMBT3904 between ways 3 and 4. In the body, the ESP32, its decoupling and EN parts, and the multiplexer with its decoupling, are in the way 1 ear. Behind the nuts are: the programming footprint, status LED, strapping resistors and the 10 µF over way 1; the programming diode and test pads over way 2; the temperature sensor's bias resistor over way 3; then, from way 4 towards way 6, the fuse, TVS diode, input caps, buck, inductor and output caps. Plan: [`placement-rev-a.png`](assets/placement-rev-a.png). The PCB X axis is seen from the parts face, so way 1 is at +X | VSUP leaves up the diode's side and VOUT up the signal side. With the divider slots gone, the gap between two nut pads is at least 3.46 mm usable on the parts face, about 12 traces, and parts can sit in it. The ring face there is inside a neighbour's swung ring keepout above about y 11, so nothing else crosses on it. Everything between the ends runs along the band behind the nuts, between and around the parts there. Each way's divider sits in the sensor band beside its SS210, outboard of the line copper, with a via from its 6.8 kΩ to the ring-face GND bus. Way 3's has the MMBT3904 there instead, so it stands above it, on the transistor's GND patch ([#30](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/30)). Ways 1–3's sense and voltage lines reach the multiplexer in the way 1 ear above way 1's nut, and ways 4–6's reach the one in the way 6 ear, mostly along the widened ring-face run at the panel edge. The module's IO pins face the end of way 1's column, so their lines drop to the ring face under the module ([#21](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/21)). The rest is for [pours](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/19) and [routing](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/20) ([#12](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/12)) |
| Tooling | tscircuit 0.0.2786, pinned exactly in `board/package.json`. `board/bun.lock` is the real pin, because the CLI it pulls in floats. Run everything with `bun run` in `board/`: `build` is the DRC gate, `fab` builds and then writes Gerbers, BOM and CPL to `board/fab/`, `placement` checks placement and renders the board without routing, and `step` writes the board solid for Fusion, also without routing. Silk is drawn in `board/lib/silkscreen.tsx`, not with tscircuit's text, which strokes too thin to print below about 1.7 mm and can't fill. Text comes from its stroke alphabet at 0.15 mm, and fills go through `silkscreengraphic` as SVG polygons. `fab` exports the Gerbers after `scripts/silk.ts` has dropped the footprints' stock silk and checked the rest: 0.15 mm lines, 0.25 mm from bare copper, 0.3 mm from the edges. `silk` runs that check without routing, so a part that moves fails it until its marks follow. The STEP has the ESP32, mux, supply and passives, but not the CC6905, SS210 or nut models, which fail to merge. Its board is a plain slab: the holes are not cut. The pinned version doesn't rotate a footprint's courtyard rect with the part, so a rotated passive or footprinter-string part can show false courtyard overlaps or hide real ones. The SS210 has a hand-written footprint with an outline courtyard for this reason; check pad gaps on the rest. Don't use the global `tsci`, which runs the global CLI, or `tsci export` alone, which skips the DRC. Use polygon pours for current paths (its autorouter narrows wide traces), keep copper out of the ESP32's antenna area, and check every rotation in JLC's assembly preview | Shown to work in a trial run with these exact parts ([#5](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/5)). The scaffold builds a placeholder board end to end ([#10](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/10)) |
