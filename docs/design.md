# Design basis

The decisions taken so far, with enough of the reasoning and numbers to judge them. The detail
and sources are in [`research/`](research/). The decisions were made in the private Hailey repo,
whose issue numbers appear in the research notes as `XDGFX/hailey#NNN`.

Prices are USD from JLCPCB/LCSC on 2026-10-09, at 10–50 quantity.

## Requirements

- Measure the current on every way of a 12-way blade fuse panel. Every way gets a sensor,
  spare ways included.
- Read every way's voltage too, so a blown or pulled fuse can raise an alert.
- About ±0.3 A is good enough. Very small loads reading as zero is accepted.
- Don't limit the panel: 30 A per way, 100 A per panel.
- Existing #8 / M4 ring terminals must fit without re-crimping. The panel cover must still fit.
- Report to Home Assistant over Wi-Fi via ESPHome.
- Accept 12 V and 24 V systems.
- Cheap, and fully assembled by JLCPCB, with no hand soldering.

## Decisions

| Area | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Topology | One yoke per 12-way ST Blade: a single board for all 12 ways, with two arms of six fingers joined by a chin past the panel's positive end. The chin carries the one ESP32, supply, ground nut and programming pads; the arms carry only each way's own parts and are 16.7 mm deep. It is drawn, placed and routed, 117.6 × 115.8 mm, in `board/index.circuit.tsx`, and every check exits 0 on it ([#92](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/92)). The outline was shortened after the printed plate was fitted, by taking 6.5 mm off the chin's outer edge: the inner edge and every hole are as printed, and it was not reprinted. This replaces identical standalone 6-way strips, two per panel. A row below describes the yoke where it says so; where it says the rest is the strip's, or has not been revised, it describes that strip, rev A, which was laid out and never ordered, and is shelved at the tag [`rev-a-strip`](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/tree/rev-a-strip): its board, pours, routes and silkscreen are read there, not in `board/`. A board for a panel of another width would restart from that tag. A Blue Sea 6 circuit is unsupported but possible, with both arms cut short ([#50](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/50), [README](../README.md#on-a-6-circuit-panel)) | Cost is close to even: $1.65 less for two panels on a 5-board, 2-assembled order and $20.25 more for one (2026-10-10). The yoke is 22.6 mm narrower on the bulkhead, is one Home Assistant device reading 12 ways on one clock, can number its ways in silk and firmware, and keeps its silkscreen in view once wired. Accepted costs: all 12 way screws out and the feed lug off to fit it, one module failing loses 12 ways, and it fits only a 12-way ST Blade. The strip's case rested on cable cost between strips, which one board doesn't have, and on about 1 W more standby, which the datasheets put at 0.09–0.33 W. The printed plate was fitted on 2026-10-11 to a Blue Sea ST Blade 12 circuit and a 6 circuit ([#50](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/50)): all 12 holes line up, the chin clears the + stud and the mounting screws, and the cover goes on. The feed lug passing over the chin and a clone were not tried, and will not be before ordering ([note](research/yoke-or-strips.md), [#40](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/40)) |
| Mechanical | A comb. Each finger, 9.5 mm wide, slides sideways into a way's pocket at screw-pad height and is clamped under the panel's own #8 screw, through a 5.4 mm round hole. Each arm of the yoke is such a comb, 16.7 mm deep outside the panel edge, where the strip needed 28.0. The first 2.3 mm of each finger's parts face, over the pocket's lip, carries 0402s (see Placement): the lip is about 1.7 mm below it on a real ST Blade's CAD, and unmeasured on a clone. Parts face down, ring face up, so rings are screwed on from above. Outline: [comb geometry](assets/comb-outline-rev-a.svg) | Barriers stand 4–8.5 mm above the screw pads, so a flat board can't lie under all six screws. A 1.6 mm finger leaves about 4 mm of thread engaged ([dimensions](research/fuse-panel-dimensions.md)). 9.5 mm and 5.4 mm also fit the Blue Sea clones: a 1.6 mm printed plate of the routed board fits both a real ST Blade and a Blue Sea clone ([#13](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/13)). That was the strip's plate; the yoke's was fitted to two Blue Sea panels and no clone ([#50](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/50)). A hole rather than an open slot, because the old rings have to come off the screws anyway; the hole keeps the tip solid and the strip captive ([#8](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/8)) |
| Fixing | The twelve way screws are the yoke's only fixing. The body has no fixing holes and the arms no stiffening bar: the yoke is handled by the chin, never by an arm. Rev A allowed an optional 3D-printed support, screwed to the bulkhead, that slid over the edge of the strip to carry its body; none is drawn for the yoke. The install steps are in the README's [Installing](../README.md#installing) section ([#101](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/101)); what they rest on: the feed lug off and all twelve way screws out, the yoke lowered on by the chin, all twelve way screws torqued first, keeping the panel's own lock washer under each, then the output rings fitted with an M4 split spring washer under each bolt (Bunnings: Pinnacle M4 stainless spring washer, 12 pack, item 0851291, AUD 2.63 on 2026-10-10; 4 mm bore, 8 mm OD, so it sits inside the ring), and the output cables tied to the bulkhead within about 100 mm. The feed lug crosses the chin and must stand clear of it, since the chin's ring face is GND under solder mask ([#91](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/91)): the README asks for a washer about 2 mm thick under the lug unless it plainly clears, Cal's default. The install notes don't say which way the feed or the ground wire runs: that is the installer's. The ring keepout is still checked only for the ground wire's ring leaving outboard ([#83](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/83)). Re-check every torque after the first warm run. On the first boards, check the torque after a hot soak at load | Both joints clamp FR4, which creeps under load when warm, so the bolts lose preload over heat cycles, and a loose 30 A joint heats itself further. A spring washer keeps the clamp up while the board settles. Mask is not insulation, so a feed lug resting on the chin could scrape through to GND with no fuse between them. What the feed's field does to the readings of ways 11 and 12, beside the chin, is not estimated: the first built yoke is bench-checked for it ([#62](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/62)). The printed plate's arms were stiff enough in the hand ([#50](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/50)); that was on the 20 mm chin, and on the 13.5 mm one they are untested ([#79](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/79)). The rest of this cell is the strip's. A feed cable laid sideways over a strip's positive end is estimated to move way 1's reading by about 0.045 A per 100 A, against 0.006 A or less led straight out or up; the estimate is unmeasured until the first built strip is bench-checked ([#57](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/57), [#62](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/62)). A conical (DIN 6796) washer would hold it better, but neither Bunnings nor Jaycar stocks one, and at 9 mm OD the M4 size only just fits the ring; the panel's CAD already shows a lock washer on every screw ([dimensions](research/fuse-panel-dimensions.md)). Six clamped screws hold the strip in the panel's plane with a wide margin, and the round holes keep it captive while any screw is in. The weak case is the body bending. Pushing down to drive an output bolt or pulling a cable puts about 2–4 N·m on the finger roots, roughly 80–160 MPa against FR4's roughly 400 MPa. No SMT standoff comes in the 14.3 mm needed, and they are extended parts (about $3.30–3.60). M3 holes with 12 × 12 mm keep-clear zones were planned, but the zones held the body 3 mm deeper and 10 mm longer at each end, and a support that grips the edge needs no hole. The support is designed later, around real strips ([#9](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/9), [#21](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/21)) |
