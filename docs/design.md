# Design basis

The decisions taken so far, with enough of the reasoning and numbers to judge them. The detail
and sources are in [`research/`](research/).

Prices are USD from JLCPCB/LCSC on 2026-10-09, at 10–50 quantity.

## Requirements

- Measure the current on every way of a 12-way blade fuse panel. Every way gets a sensor,
  spare ways included.
- Read every way's voltage too, so a blown or pulled fuse can raise an alert.
- About ±0.3 A is good enough. Very small loads reading as zero is accepted.
- Don't limit the panel: 30 A per way, 100 A per panel.
- Existing #8 / M4 ring terminals must fit without re-crimping. The panel cover must still fit.
- Report to Home Assistant over Wi-Fi via ESPHome.
- Accept 12 V and 24 V systems.
- Cheap, and fully assembled by JLCPCB, with no hand soldering.

## Decisions

| Area | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Topology | One yoke per 12-way ST Blade: a single board for all 12 ways, with two arms of six fingers joined by a chin past the panel's positive end. The chin carries the one ESP32, supply, ground nut and programming pads; the arms carry only each way's own parts and are 16.7 mm deep. It is drawn, placed and routed, 117.6 × 115.8 mm, in `board/index.circuit.tsx`, and every check exits 0 on it ([#92](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/92)). The outline was shortened after the printed plate was fitted, by taking 6.5 mm off the chin's outer edge: the inner edge and every hole are as printed, and it was not reprinted. Five were built from the first order, and two went into Cal's install in early November 2026. The yoke replaced two standalone 6-way strips per panel: rev A, laid out and never ordered, and shelved at the tag [`rev-a-strip`](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/tree/rev-a-strip), where a board for a panel of another width would restart. A Blue Sea 6 circuit is unsupported but possible, with both arms cut short ([#50](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/50), [README](../README.md#on-a-6-circuit-panel)) | Cost is close to even: $1.65 less for two panels on a 5-board, 2-assembled order and $20.25 more for one (2026-10-10). The yoke is 22.6 mm narrower on the bulkhead, is one Home Assistant device reading 12 ways on one clock, can number its ways in silk and firmware, and keeps its silkscreen in view once wired. Accepted costs: all 12 way screws out and the feed lug off to fit it, one module failing loses 12 ways, and it fits only a 12-way ST Blade. The strip's case rested on cable cost between strips, which one board doesn't have, and on about 1 W more standby, which the datasheets put at 0.09–0.33 W. The printed plate was fitted on 2026-10-11 to a Blue Sea ST Blade 12 circuit and a 6 circuit ([#50](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/50)): all 12 holes line up, the chin clears the + stud and the mounting screws, and the cover goes on. The built yokes in Cal's install confirm those dimensions. A clone has not been tried ([note](research/yoke-or-strips.md), [#40](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/40)) |
| Mechanical | A comb. Each finger, 9.5 mm wide, slides sideways into a way's pocket at screw-pad height and is clamped under the panel's own #8 screw, through a 5.4 mm round hole. Each arm of the yoke is such a comb, 16.7 mm deep outside the panel edge. The first 2.3 mm of each finger's parts face, over the pocket's lip, carries 0402s (see Placement): the lip is about 1.7 mm below it on a real ST Blade's CAD, and unmeasured on a clone. Parts face down, ring face up, so rings are screwed on from above | Barriers stand 4–8.5 mm above the screw pads, so a flat board can't lie under all six screws. A 1.6 mm finger leaves about 4 mm of thread engaged ([dimensions](research/fuse-panel-dimensions.md)). 9.5 mm and 5.4 mm also fit the Blue Sea clones: a 1.6 mm printed plate of rev A, with the same fingers, fitted both a real ST Blade and a Blue Sea clone ([#13](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/13)). The yoke's plate was fitted to two Blue Sea panels and no clone ([#50](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/50)). A hole rather than an open slot, because the old rings have to come off the screws anyway; the hole keeps the tip solid and the yoke captive ([#8](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/8)) |
| Fixing | The twelve way screws are the yoke's only fixing. The body has no fixing holes and the arms no stiffening bar: the yoke is handled by the chin, never by an arm. The install steps are in the README's [Installing](../README.md#installing) section ([#101](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/101)); what they rest on: the feed lug off and all twelve way screws out, the yoke lowered on by the chin, all twelve way screws torqued first, keeping the panel's own lock washer under each, then the output rings fitted with an M4 split spring washer under each bolt (Bunnings: Pinnacle M4 stainless spring washer, 12 pack, item 0851291, AUD 2.63 on 2026-10-10; 4 mm bore, 8 mm OD, so it sits inside the ring), and the output cables tied to the bulkhead within about 100 mm. The feed lug crosses the chin and must stand clear of it, since the chin's ring face is GND under solder mask ([#91](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/91)): the README asks for a washer about 2 mm thick under the lug unless it plainly clears, Cal's default. Cal's lugs cleared without one, but lugs vary. The install notes don't say which way the feed or the ground wire runs: that is the installer's. The ring keepout is still checked only for the ground wire's ring leaving outboard ([#83](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/83)). Re-check every torque after the first warm run. On the first boards, check the torque after a hot soak at load | Both joints clamp FR4, which creeps under load when warm, so the bolts lose preload over heat cycles, and a loose 30 A joint heats itself further. A spring washer keeps the clamp up while the board settles. Mask is not insulation, so a feed lug resting on the chin could scrape through to GND with no fuse between them. A conical (DIN 6796) washer would hold it better, but neither Bunnings nor Jaycar stocks one, and at 9 mm OD the M4 size only just fits the ring; the panel's CAD already shows a lock washer on every screw ([dimensions](research/fuse-panel-dimensions.md)). The round holes keep the yoke captive while any screw is in. Measured on yoke B, with the feed leaving beside the chin on the even side, way 12 moves −0.24 ± 0.05 A per 100 A of feed current and way 11 −0.03, scaled from 8 A steps ([#62](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/62)); the firmware leaves it uncorrected (see Firmware). The arms on the 13.5 mm chin are stiff enough in service: two built yokes are in Cal's install ([#79](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/79)) |
| Body | The yoke's arms, as placed ([#80](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/80)): 16.7 deep from the panel edge, with one row of output nuts on 13.36 pitch, centres 10.7 out, each land 1.3 from the outboard edge. Sensor band 1.0–6.6 out. Each neck between two nuts holds a diode, so the 2 mm lane for a printed divider is dropped on the arms. Nothing is placed behind the nuts, where VSUP's trunk runs ([#85](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/85)), or in the 6.68 past ways 1–2 at the open end. The chin, as placed ([#79](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/79)): 13.5 deep on the centreline, with 3 mm corners. The module's antenna stays 15 mm from way 11's nut land, which fixes how far down the arm the module sits, and its chin-side pin row needs about 4 mm to the outer edge; those two set the depth. J1's clamp box needs 9.5 and the ground nut 12, so about 12 is the floor. The antenna stands 6 mm past the odd arm's outboard edge, so the yoke needs 22.7 beside the panel there and 16.7 elsewhere | A smaller body costs less and fits more panels, but no depth is a target in itself. One row of nuts keeps the arms shallower than staggered rows. A single ring touches its neighbour only after swinging 39°, or 50° when the whole bundle swings, so barriers are optional ([#15](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/15)). A divider that grips the edge would need no lane or hole. Wi-Fi works at 4–5 m in Cal's install; longer ranges are untested |
| Sensor | Cross Chip CC6905S8-3FB030 (C41421490), $0.56: 3.3 V, ±30 A, 44 mV/A, 0.6 mΩ max (0.45 W at 30 A) | Cheapest stocked 30 A part, and it runs from 3.3 V, so no 5 V rail or dividers. It has no factory temperature compensation; see Accuracy ([sensors](research/way-monitor-current-sensor.md)) |
| ADC | ESP32-C3-WROOM-02-N4 (C2934560, extended, 4 MB, −40 to 85 °C, chip revision v0.4) on Espressif's own lands, 1.5 × 0.9, plus two 74HC4067PW 16:1 multiplexers (C179326, extended, $0.48 on 2026-10-10), one for each arm: mux 1 on the chin and mux 2 on the even arm's root ([#79](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/79)). Each reads its arm's six ways, current and voltage, and the arm's temperature sensor; mux 1 also reads the chin's. That is 13 and 14 of 16 channels, on two ADC pins, GPIO4 and GPIO3, at the 12 dB range. The WROOM-02-H4 (C2944070, 105 °C) and the DOIT ESPC3-20 fit the same lands and can be swapped in at order time. Derating that goes with the 85 °C module: 30 A a way and 100 A a panel up to 40 °C in free air; above 40 °C, or in a closed locker, 20 A a way. Firmware warns when the board passes about 85 °C | The C3 has only 4 clean ADC1 channels with Wi-Fi on. On the yoke every multiplexer is on the chin, and two 74HC4067PWs take a third of the area of four CD4051Bs, use one ADC pin fewer and are inside their rated supply at 3.3 V, for one extended fee and about $1.40 a yoke ([#65](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/65)). The ADC stays at 12 dB, good to 2.5 V: the even arm's sense lines are divided to fit and the odd arm's fit whole, so each way has its own zero and gain in firmware and the two arms' slopes have opposite signs ([#73](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/73)). The ESP32-C2 would save about $0.70 a module, but its stock is thin and its ESPHome support weaker ([ADC](research/way-monitor-adc.md)). JLC will not place the ESP32-C3-MINI-1 under Economic PCBA: its pads are under the body. The castellated WROOM-02 is Economic, with the same chip and pins; fees and four modules come to $41.29 an order against $90.39 on Standard with the MINI-1 (2026-10-10, [#41](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/41), [#43](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/43), [#54](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/54)). Cal accepted the N4's 85 °C rating with the WROOM-02, where the strip's thermal estimate had moved to a 105 °C module ([#17](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/17), [#18](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/18)); the derating's 40 °C line is where the module's margin runs out too. The yoke's estimate ([#74](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/74)): the derating holds as written, and the module where it was placed, 11.2 mm from way 11's sensor, is at about 81 °C in the worst case (see Placement). Espressif's lands, not JLC's 2.0 × 1.0, leave 0.6 between lands; they cost 0.25 mm of exposed toe, which matters only for hand rework ([#58](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/58)) |
| Output terminal | Sinhoo SMT M4 nut (C2916378), $0.17. The ring clamps onto a copper pad. Its land is Ø9.37 round a Ø6.4 hole | Assembled with everything else. The current flows ring to pad, so the nut's missing current rating barely matters. Torque about 1.0–1.2 N·m. Sinhoo gives no land; Ø9.37 is PEM's minimum for its SMTSO-M4 ([sheet](https://damlite.pemnet.com/SMTSO-M4-3ET_pdf_1.pdf), checked 2026-10-10), which this nut copies: the same Ø8.74 ±0.13 flange and Ø6.22 pilot. Lever terminals cost $2+ and would mean re-terminating ([terminals](research/way-monitor-terminals-and-copper.md)) |
| Copper | The yoke's, in `board/lib/copper.tsx` ([#84](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/84)): solid pours on both layers, masked, with no thermal relief, the same on all twelve ways, in the column's own coordinates so the even arm's are the odd arm's mirrored. On either arm the sensor's two IP pins nearest the panel are the line's and the two outboard the load's, so the nets don't change places; only the sensor's own IP+ and IP− do. Line: the finger, 8.9 wide on both faces. The ring face's stops 0.3 short of the panel edge. Under the finger's root, from 2.9 into the panel, the parts face keeps only a neck 3.8 wide, between the last way's caps and this way's divider, which keep the root out to the finger's edges on each side. Past the panel edge it is a slab up to 3.65 out, over the line's IP pins, and a strip 0.8 wide under the sensor's body, beside the IP pins, out to the diode's anode. Load: one rectangle on the parts face, 6.4 wide, from the load's IP pins (3.95 out) to the nut's centre line, and on the ring face 12.4 wide from 5.8 out to 0.3 short of the outboard edge, its outboard corners cut for the neighbours' ring keepouts swung 20°, and ways 1 and 2's for the open end's chamfer. Stitched with 0.3 mm tented vias: 12 in the neck and 4 between the load's pins and the nut, 0.4 outside its land; the clamp hole and the nut's barrel join the faces too. Bare copper (HASL) only where the panel clamps: on the parts face from 3.0 mm into the panel to the tip, and the washer ring on the ring face. Left free: the ring face from the panel edge to 5.8 out, for the lanes and whatever runs along the edge; the parts face behind the nuts, for VSUP's trunk; the parts face between a nut and the next sensor's signal pins; and a 0.6 strip along the panel edge under the sensor, where the way's voltage line leaves its divider. The divider's own line pad, 0.3 from the neck, is left for the routing to join. No GND is poured on the arms, where it is a bus in the channel (see Routing); the chin's ring face is one GND pour ([#91](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/91)). Path resistance, clamp to line pins plus load pins to nut land, solved on the built pours at 0.491 mΩ a square: 1.09 mΩ a way (line 0.90, load 0.19), every way within 0.01. Solved again on the routed board, with the GND vias' slots in the ring face's line copper ([#92](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/92)): still 1.09, the line 0.008 up at most. That is 0.19 over the 0.90 the thermal estimate assumed ([#74](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/74)), about +4 K on its +67 K. The divider's room costs about 0.17 of it. Accepted as drawn at 1 oz ([#87](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/87)): the dividers stay under the finger roots, the ring face's line copper stays 0.3 short of the panel edge, and the lanes are drawn 0.16 mm wide so 2 oz stays open. The +71 K is scaled from the estimate, not re-estimated. On the first yoke, put 30 A through one way with 15 A on each neighbour: the next revision goes to 2 oz if the sensor's package would pass 105 °C in 40 °C ambient. `keepout` is clear straight out and swung 20°; swung 39° each ring's tongue reaches its neighbours' load copper | The line is the weak half: the 0402s under the finger's root ([#80](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/80)) leave it a neck, and the ring face can't help past the panel edge, where the lanes are, so the last 4 mm to the IP pins is one face. Letting the ring face's line copper run out to 1.15 takes only 0.09 mΩ off and blocks the panel edge on that face. The diode stands outboard of the load's pins, so the line reaches it under the sensor: that strip carries only the board's own supply current, tens of milliamps, 1 mm from the sensor's primary, and whether the sensor reads any of it is unmeasured. The load is short because the nut's land starts 1 mm from the pins. IPC-2152 widths for a long 30 A trace don't apply to a path this short. The finger can't take the 9.5 mm asked for in [#17](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/17), because 9.5 is the finger's own width |
| Power | Every way feeds the supply through its own SS210 diode (C14996, basic), tapped before its sensor. Then a 1 A SMD fuse (Littelfuse 0466001, C151135), an SMBJ33A TVS (C19077586, preferred) and a TI LMR51610X buck (C20539658), 65 V rated. Ground is a wire from an M4 SMT nut to the negative bus. No jumper. On the yoke that is twelve way diodes into one supply node, one fuse, one buck and one ground nut, on the chin ([#78](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/78)). Two more SS210s feed the same node, ahead of the fuse: one from J1's VDD and one from the USB socket's VBUS, so 5 V from either runs the board off the panel and neither is back-fed from a live panel. The 3.3 V load is about 0.51 A at worst, twelve sensors at 13 mA and the module's 350 mA transmit peak, on a 1 A buck. Each sensor has 100 nF at VCC; the module has 10 µF and 100 nF, and each multiplexer 100 nF. The buck's 2.2 µF input capacitor, C1 (C50254), is rated 50 V on a node the TVS lets rise to 53.3 V, under TI's twice-the-input guidance: accepted on 2026-10-12, since a LiFePO4 bank doesn't surge near 50 V and the 100 nF beside it is 100 V | The yoke runs while any of its ways has a live fuse, and its own draw stays out of every way's reading. The diodes also block reverse polarity. JLC has no basic or preferred fuse or PTC, so the fuse's $3 fee is unavoidable. The XL1509 was dropped because no TVS stays off at 29.2 V (24 V LiFePO4) and also clamps below its 45 V limit. For the same two fees, the LMR51610X costs about $0.80 more and is about 13 points more efficient ([#6](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/6), [power](research/way-monitor-power-supply.md)) |
| Pin map and programming | The yoke's schematic is drawn, in `board/index.circuit.tsx` ([#78](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/78)). Two 74HC4067PWs share selects on GPIO7, 10, 5 and 6 for S0 to S3 ([#95](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/95)), with E̅ to GND and 100 nF each. Mux 1 has its common on GPIO4 and reads the odd arm, ways 1–11; mux 2 has its common on GPIO3 and reads the even arm, ways 2–12. Each common has 10 nF, to stand at its ADC pin. Mux 1's channels follow its routing ([#90](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/90)): Y7 down to Y2 are voltage then sense for ways 1, 3 and 5, Y8 up to Y13 voltage then sense for ways 7, 9 and 11, Y14 the odd arm's temperature sensor and Y1 the chin's; Y0 and Y15 are spare and tied to GND. Mux 2's follow its routing too ([#89](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/89)): Y9, Y10 and Y11 are the voltages of ways 2, 4 and 6 and Y13, Y14 and Y15 those of ways 8, 10 and 12, Y8 is the even arm's temperature sensor, and the divided sense lines are Y5 for way 12, Y6 for way 2, Y7 for way 4, Y4 for way 6, Y3 for way 8 and Y2 for way 10; Y0, Y1 and Y12 are spare and tied to GND, Y12 so that the common's line can pass its pin. The schematic is the reference for firmware. The even arm is the mirrored one ([#65](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/65)): its current runs IP+ to IP−, so its sense lines rise with load, to 2.97 V at 30 A, and each is divided by 0.820 at mux 2, 22 kΩ (C25768, basic) over 100 kΩ (C25741, basic), for 1.35 V at rest and 2.43 V at 30 A ([#73](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/73)). The odd arm's are undivided. Which arm is mirrored is one line of the schematic, and the arm's layout confirmed it ([#80](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/80)). Vn is a 100 kΩ / 6.8 kΩ divider from way n's line copper beside its SS210 anode, before the sensor, so its current stays out of the reading; it reads each way's voltage, shows a blown or pulled fuse, and gives the supply voltage without the diode drop, so there is no separate supply divider ([#29](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/29)). Every module pin but GPIO0 is used: GPIO4 and GPIO3 for the two commons, GPIO7, 10, 5 and 6 for the selects, GPIO1 for the status LED, GPIO8 for the fault LED, GPIO9 for boot, GPIO18 and GPIO19 for USB, and RXD and TXD for J1. GPIO0, the last clean ADC1 pin, ends at the module's pad, and GPIO2 has only its pull-up. EN, GPIO2, GPIO8 and GPIO9 each have a 10 kΩ pull-up, and EN 1 µF at its land. GPIO8's pull-up is what holds it high at boot: the fault LED alone would leave it a diode drop short. Test pads for 3V3, the supply node and GND. Flashed with an Espressif ESP-PROG through J1, with its TXD and RXD leads swapped: J1's TXD pad is on the module's RXD and its RXD pad on the module's TXD, and the ESP-PROG's header names its pins from the ESP's side (its schematic puts the FT2232's TXD on header pin 5, ESP_RXD0). Found in the pre-order review on 2026-10-12 and left as drawn, Cal's call: the swap is two jumper leads, and the first flash goes in over USB. J1 is six SMT pads on a 2×3 2.54 mm grid, on the parts face only, reached by a 2.54 mm double-row 6P pogo clamp slid on from the board edge; it follows [#77](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/77) and stands on the chin's centreline ([#79](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/79)). Its VDD (ESP-PROG on 5 V) feeds the supply node through an SS210. Rev B also has ([#75](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/75)): a 16-pin USB-C socket (HRO TYPE-C-31-M-12, C165948, extended, $0.19 on 2026-10-11) on the chin's edge, on the module's native USB at GPIO18 and GPIO19, with a 5.1 kΩ (C23186, basic) from each CC pin to GND and VBUS feeding the supply node through its own SS210; no USB ESD part. The socket is 8.94 × 7.35 and 3.26 high, overhangs the edge by about 0.5, and has four shell stakes in plated slots that stop inside the board. J1 stays as the recovery route. The status LED on GPIO1 becomes green (KT-0805G, C2297, basic), and a red fault LED (KT-0603R, C2286) on GPIO8, wired from 3V3 so it lights when the pin is low, is steady while something on the board is wrong (a blown fuse, over-range or over-temperature), not when a load is high. It also blinks while a zero is being taken and in install mode ([#108](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/108), [#113](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/113)). The expansion pads that ticket let in, J3, are dropped ([#95](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/95)): they stood on the parts face, against the bulkhead once fitted, and were what the module's lines could not get past. Still no buttons, and no buzzer, LED per way or feed current sensor. Flash off the panel, or with the ground wire fitted; update over Wi-Fi on the panel | Wi-Fi updates make a wired port a first-flash and recovery tool, so it shouldn't cost a part fee. Every USB-C socket and pin header at JLC is extended. On rev B the socket is let in all the same: on native USB it is one extended fee, about $3 an order, and three parts, less than the pogo clamp J1 needs, and it flashes from any laptop. The feed's sum is already the twelve ways, a buzzer is the tallest part and Home Assistant alerts anyway, and the only addressable LED Economic PCBA places needs 5 V. The ESP-PROG's auto-reset makes BOOT and RESET buttons unnecessary. A product would buy pre-flashed modules instead. J1 started on the strip as a through-hole lock footprint, but its pads were then bare on the ring face under way 1's ring keepout; nowhere on the body is clear of every keepout ([#7](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/7), [ESP32-C3 research](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/4), [#26](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/26)) |
| Ring keepout | Sized for an M4 ring for 4–6 mm² cable at most: a Ø10 ring with a 10 mm wide tongue running outboard past the board edge. Inside a way's ring keepout the ring face carries only that way's own copper; every other net, GND included, crosses on the parts face. The straight-out keepout is the minimum. The goal is the keepout swung ±20° about the nut, and ±39° where it costs nothing. On the yoke ([#83](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/83)) the keepout is the same shape on all thirteen nuts, with the tongue running outboard, away from the centreline: towards −X on the odd arm and +X on the even arm. The board edge is 6.0 past an output nut's centre and 7.8 past the ground nut's. The ground nut's keepout takes GND as its own net, and its wire is assumed to leave outboard like the even arm's cables. The lane behind the nuts lies in every straight-out keepout, so VSUP's trunk there is on the parts face. Each neck's diode has its VSUP pad 0.3 inside the two keepouts beside it at ±20° and 1.9 at ±39°, and mux 2 has one select pad 0.8 inside the ground nut's at ±39°: neither pad takes a via where it stands | A 30 A way never needs more than 4 mm² cable, and most ways use 1.5–2.5 mm² with smaller rings. ±39° stacks the largest ring on the largest swing, and it cost J1 its through-hole pads and the ring face between the nuts. Mask isn't insulation: a ring that turns while its nut is torqued can scrape it, and a way's 12–24 V landing on GND is a dead short through the fuse. 39° is where a ring meets its neighbour, and that contact only joins two fused ways, not a short to GND ([#26](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/26)) |
| Accuracy | A zero per way, taken whenever the way is dead (below 2 V for 5 s, then the mean of the next 10 s), since then no current can flow, and again every hour it stays dead, which Cal added after review of [#117](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/117). Never when it merely sits steady near zero, which would hide the standby drain the board exists to find. A way that is never dead in service gets its zero at install, with its fuse pulled ([#113](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/113)). Until then it borrows the median of the stored zeros on its own arm, or the nominal 1.65 V if that arm has none: Cal decided this after testing on yoke B, where every way zeroed 7–16 mV above nominal, an offset the whole arm seems to share. Gain is the CC6905's nominal 44 mV/A, with a trim per way that starts at 1.0 and is set over the API within 0.90–1.10. Nothing works the trims out yet: at ±3 % the nominal gain is inside ±0.3 A below about 10 A ([#110](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/110)). Three temperature sensors, one on the chin and one on each arm, each on a multiplexer channel: a diode-connected MMBT3904 (C20526, basic, $0.0115) biased at about 265 µA by a 10 kΩ from 3.3 V, read as Vbe (0.59 V at 25 °C as read through the ADC on yoke B, −2 mV/°C).
