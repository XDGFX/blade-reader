# Fit dimensions of the 6-way ST Blade

Whether a yoke made for the Blue Sea ST Blade 12-way (5026) could be cut down to three fingers
per arm and fitted to the 6-way ST Blade. Checked on 2026-10-11.

**The 6-way is the 12-way with three way pitches taken out of the middle.** The positive end,
the way pitch, the width and the row spacing carry over, so the three fingers nearest the chin
land on the 6-way's screws. **The other three fingers cannot stay on uncut**, on either variant:
the first hits a wall past ways 1–2, and on the 5025 the next two land in the negative bus
pocket.

Each figure is marked **DS** (printed on Blue Sea's dimensioned drawing), **scaled** (pixels
measured on that drawing, about 0.6–0.7 mm per pixel, so ±0.7 mm at best), **inferred** (12-way
CAD figures from [`fuse-panel-dimensions.md`](fuse-panel-dimensions.md) shifted by the DS length
difference, or read off a product photo) or **not found**. No CAD model of a 6-way was found, and
no panel was measured.

- **5025 − 5028 = 31.55**, in both. The 5028 loses the negative bus pocket (33.0 long on the

- The 5030 drawing prints the hole spacing as `3.881"` / `95.58mm`. 3.881 in is 98.58 mm, so the
  metric figure is a typo.

The same pitch, width, hole spacing, screw and drawing style across all three leave little
doubt it is one design stretched, but the pad height (14.33) and pocket width (11.11) rest on
that and nothing more.

## 2. Way arrangement

- **Three ways per side**, paired across the panel: ways 1–2, 3–4, 5–6. Numbers are moulded in
  and printed on the drawings (DS).
- **Ways 5–6 sit nearest the + stud.** The + stud is on the centreline at the end beside ways
  5–6; on the 5025 the negative bus and − stud are at the end beside ways 1–2 (DS, photos [6]).
  On the 12-way it is ways 11–12 at the + stud. The 5028 has no stud at its other end.
- **The positive end is unchanged.** Scaled, the positive-end mounting hole to the nearest way
  screw is 14.7 on the 5028 and 14.4 on the 5025, against 15.03 on the 12-way CAD. With the
  12.87 end margin the same on all three (DS), the way screws sit at **27.83, 41.19 and 54.55**
  from the positive end (inferred), which are the 12-way's first three positions. + stud at
  about 11.3 along (inferred; not dimensioned).

So a yoke's chin and the three fingers nearest it on each arm line up with a 6-way as they do
with the 12-way. Those fingers serve the 12-way's ways 11–12, 9–10, 7–8 and the 6-way's ways
5–6, 3–4, 1–2.

## 3. Past the third way

Along is from the positive end. The unused fingers would sit at 67.91, 81.27 and 94.63; a 9.5
finger covers 63.2–72.7, 76.5–86.0 and 89.9–99.4.

| Along | 5025 | 5028 | Backing |
|---|---|---|---|
| 60.1 onwards | Wall, 60.1–68.1, about 19.4–21 high on the 12-way | Raised block, about 60–72, as tall as the barriers or taller | 5025 inferred from CAD; 5028 scaled (12 mm long) and photo |
| Next | Negative bus pocket, 68.1–101.1: three screws per side at 73.46, 84.54, 95.61, pad top 14.32, open to the long side, no barriers | End deck, about 72–92.76, with the mounting hole at 79.9 | 5025 inferred, scaled pitch 11.06 and pocket 33.5 agree; 5028 scaled |
| End | Mounting hole at 111.4, − stud at about 112.9, end at 124.31 | End at 92.76. No stud | Lengths DS, the rest inferred |

**An uncut arm does not fit either panel.**

- **Fourth finger, both panels:** it meets the wall or block past ways 1–2, which stands 5 mm or
  more above the pad plane. It stops the board seating. Blocked.
- **Fifth and sixth fingers, 5025:** they land in the negative bus pocket at the same height as
  the way pads, on or beside the negative screws (the sixth finger's hole is 1.0 from a
  negative screw's centre). That puts way copper on the negative bus and covers terminals the
  install needs. Blocked, and a short hazard.
- **Fifth and sixth fingers, 5028:** the fifth hangs over the end deck and the mounting screw;
  the sixth is mostly past the end of the panel. If the end deck is at 12.7 as on the 12-way,
  the fifth clears it by about 1.6, but that height is not known for the 5028, and the finger
  covers the mounting screw. Not a clean fit even with the fourth finger removed.
- **Cutting the fingers only and leaving the arm's outboard part** clears the mouldings if
  nothing protrudes past the 6-way's side edges, as on the 12-way (inferred). On the 5025 it
  would then run across the open side of the negative bus pocket, where the negative wires
  leave. On the 5028 it would overhang the end by up to 7 mm plus whatever the arm carries
  past the last finger.
- **Where to cut:** between the third and fourth fingers, at about 61 along. The cut removes
  whatever the arm carries from there to its end, so a yoke meant to be cut down needs
  everything for the first three ways, and the supply and module, on the chin side of that
  line.

## 4. Mounting holes and cover

- **Mounting holes:** four, on 63.50 × 98.58 (5025, 5030) or 63.50 × 67.03 (5028), for #8 or M4
  (DS). The positive-end pair is where the 12-way's is, 12.87 from the end (inferred from the
  symmetric margin).
- **Cover:** 38.57 high on all three (DS), a clear snap-on cover with a push-button latch [4].
  The side elevations look alike. Skirt depth, side cutouts and wall thickness: **not found**,
  as for the 12-way. The cover is drawn offset in every product photo, so the photos do not
  show whether a board passes out under it.

## An aside on the 12-way

The 5026 drawing shows **two columns of three negative screws per side**, 12 in all, where the
5025 has one column per side. `fuse-panel-dimensions.md` records six, three per side, though its
model has 24 screws. The inner column may have been missed there. It does not bear on the
fingers.

## Still unknown

- 6-way pad height, pocket width, barrier and wall heights, and the 5028's end-deck height: no
  CAD model or dimension found.
- Row spacing of the way screws on a 6-way: not dimensioned; rests on the same 84.20 width.
- The cover's skirt on any ST Blade.
- A dimensioned drawing for the 5033.
- A 6-way in hand and a printed plate would settle all of these.
