# The feed cable's stray field at the current sensors

Answers [#57](https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader/issues/57): is the CC6905 a single-Hall or a
differential part, and what error does the main feed cable's field put on the sensors nearest
the positive end?

Every figure is marked **DS** (stated in a datasheet or application note), **calc** (worked out
here) or **assumed**. Nothing here was measured.

## Executive summary

**The CC6905 is a differential part, by its datasheet's own words, so the feed cable's field is
not a problem worth a board change.** The datasheet gives no number for how well it rejects an
outside field, so the size of what is left is an estimate built on Allegro's figures for the same
kind of part.

- **Worst plausible route:** the feed leaves sideways, over the end of a strip. At 100 A the way
  nearest the positive end reads up to about **0.05 A** wrong (calc, on assumed part figures).
  That is a tenth of the ±0.45 A offset drift and a sixth of the ±0.3 A target. The next way sees
  about half of it.
- **Blue Sea's route, straight out off the end:** under 0.01 A.
- **Had the part been single-Hall,** the sideways route would have cost about 0.9 A on that way,
  three times the target. That is why the datasheet's word mattered.

| Option | Cost | What it buys | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nothing | None | Error stays at 0.05 A or less per 100 A | Acceptable on its own |
| **Install note on feed routing** | One line in the install notes | Under 0.01 A on the preferred route, and it rules out the one route that is bad for any part: the feed lying on a strip | **Recommended**, with the bench check below |
| Firmware correction from the panel's total | Firmware, an install-time calibration per route, and a total that one standalone strip does not have | Removes an error already below the noise | Runner-up, only if the bench check shows more than about 0.1 A per 100 A |
| Shield | A steel or mu-metal part, hand fitted, not a JLC part | Little: it also bends the sensor's own field and adds hysteresis | No |
| Different sensor | A new part type, a re-proved footprint | Nothing, unless the bench check shows the CC6905 is not what its datasheet says | No |

**Recommendation.** Change nothing on the strip or the yoke. Add one line to the install notes:
lead the feed straight out off the positive end, or straight up from the stud, and never lay it
across or along a strip. On the first assembled strip, do the one bench check in
[Verifying it](#verifying-it), because the datasheet claims the structure and gives no figure.

## What the datasheet says

Cross Chip, *CC6905* datasheet, DATASHEET-CC6905-EN-rev1.3 (2024-09-18), 56 pages, the copy LCSC
serves for C41421490.[^ds]

| Where | Wording | Mark |
|---|---|---|
| p. 1, Features | "Differential Hall structure, strong resistance to external magnetic interference" | DS |
| p. 1, General Description | "The Hall circuit based on BiCMOS process integrates a differential Hall sensor, chopper amplifier, variable gain amplifier, …" | DS |
| p. 1, General Description | "The differential common-mode suppression circuit integrated in CC6905 can make the chip output unaffected by external interference magnetic signals." | DS |
| p. 41, Function Description | "the internal integrated differential common mode suppression circuit can make the chip output not affected by external interference magnetic signal, and only measure the magnetic field generated by the input current" | DS |

**What it does not say.** There is no common-mode field rejection figure, no mA/G figure, no
coupling factor in G/A, no Hall plate spacing and no layout guidance about nearby conductors. A
text search of all 56 pages for "rejection", "CMFR", "gauss", "G/A", "adjacent" and "nearby"
finds nothing. The CC6905S8-3FB030 table on p. 11 has no stray-field row. "Unaffected" is a
marketing claim, not a specification.

The function block diagram on p. 2 agrees with the wording: it draws two Hall elements, labelled
"Differential Hall", feeding one rotary chopper. One sits inside the loop of the IP+ to IP−
conductor and the other outside it, side by side on the line running from the IP pins towards
the signal pins (DS, a schematic drawing, not to scale).

**Confidence.** High that the part is differential: the datasheet says so four times and nothing
contradicts it. Low on how good the rejection is: every number below for the differential case
rests on Allegro's figures for its own parts, not on Cross Chip's.

## Which way the sensor looks

A Hall plate in an SOIC-8 current sensor senses the field normal to the package, which is the
field normal to the strip: straight up and down from the bulkhead. Only that component counts.

- A current running **parallel to the strip's plane** (a cable lying sideways, off the end or
  along the panel) makes a field that circles it. At a sensor beside it, that field is mostly
  normal to the strip. This is the bad direction.
- A current running **normal to the strip** (the stud itself, or a feed rising straight up from
  it) makes a field that lies wholly in the strip's plane. Its normal component is zero at every
  sensor (calc, exact for a straight vertical run).

A differential part subtracts two Hall plates a short distance apart. Two things get through
(Allegro AN269123[^allegro], DS):

1. **Mismatch.** "Hall plate matching on a single die is normally better than 1%", so a uniform
   field is rejected by about 40 dB.
2. **Gradient.** A field that differs between the two plates is not common-mode. Allegro's parts
   have plates "around 0.6 to 1 mm" apart.

Allegro's parts couple at "around 10 to 15 G/A". So, per amp of error:

- single-Hall: error = B<sub>n</sub> / CF
- differential: error ≤ (0.01 × B<sub>n</sub> + |∇B<sub>n</sub>| × d) / CF

with B<sub>n</sub> the normal field. Taken for the CC6905 as **CF = 10 G/A, d = 1 mm, 1 %
matching, and the plates lying along the gradient: all assumed**, and all at the pessimistic end
of Allegro's ranges. The gradient term turns out about four times the mismatch term, so Allegro's
"1 % of the single-Hall error" rule of thumb, used in the earlier note, understates it. Its
10 mA becomes about 45 mA.

On the strip the sensor has its IP pins towards −X, so the package's short axis lies along the
panel. That is also the direction of the gradient from a sideways feed. The p. 2 block diagram
draws the two plates along that short axis, so the worst case is taken to apply (inferred from a
schematic drawing, not a die drawing).

## Working, for the sideways route

The feed leaves the stud across the panel, towards the strip in question, and passes the end way
at along 11.33.

- Offset along the panel: 27.83 − 11.33 = 16.5 mm. Offset in height: 22.4 − 13.6 = 8.8 mm.
- Distance: r = √(16.5² + 8.8²) = **18.7 mm** (calc). This is the earlier note's "about 18 mm".
- B = μ₀I / 2πr = 2 × I[A] / r[mm] gauss = 2 × 100 / 18.7 = **10.7 G** at 100 A (calc).
- Normal component: B × 16.5 / 18.7 = **9.4 G**, 88 % of the whole field (calc).
- The cable starts at the stud rather than running both ways, which takes about 4 % off. The lug
  being lower than the cable puts most of that back.
- Gradient of the normal component along the panel: 2I(h² − x²) / (x² + h²)² =
  200 × (77 − 272) / 350² = **0.32 G/mm** by hand, 0.36 G/mm with the lug modelled (calc).
- Single-Hall: 9.4 / 10 = **0.94 A**. Differential: (0.01 × 9.4 + 0.36 × 1) / 10 =
  **0.045 A** (calc on the assumed part figures).

The table below comes from a numerical Biot–Savart sum over each route as straight segments,
which agrees with the hand figures above.

**How fast it falls off.** For the sideways route the 2nd way sees 61 % of the 1st, the 3rd
41 %, the 6th 19 %. The differential error falls faster (56 %, 31 %, 10 %), because the gradient
falls as the square of the distance. A route that runs along the panel does not fall off at all.

**The route that is bad for any part.** A feed lying across a strip, a few millimetres over a
sensor, puts 10–20 G on it with a gradient of 2 G/mm (calc), which is up to about 0.2 A even on
a differential part. With the cover fitted the cable cannot cross the fuses, so this needs a
deliberate bad route, but the install note should exclude it.

| **Feed cable, sideways, 1st way, 100 A** | | **0.045 A, upper estimate** |
| Feed cable, straight out off the end, 1st way, 100 A | | 0.006 A |

The worst plausible route costs about 15 % of the target on one way of each strip, at full
panel load. It scales with the panel's total, so at a 30 A load it is 0.014 A. Zeroing does not
remove it, as the ticket says, but it is too small to need removing.

## Options

**Nothing.** Costs nothing. The error is real but under 0.05 A per 100 A on the worst plausible
route. The risk left is that the CC6905's rejection is worse than Allegro's figures suggest.

**An install note on feed routing (recommended).** One line: lead the feed straight out off the
positive end or straight up from the stud; sideways is acceptable; never lay it across or along
a strip. It costs nothing and agrees with the antenna note's rule, which already allows straight
out and sideways and bans the diagonal over the antenna corner. With a strip on each side, or
the yoke, a sideways feed always passes over one strip's end, so straight out is the route to
prefer.

**Firmware correction.** The error on each way is a fixed fraction of the panel's total for a
given cable route, so it could be subtracted. Costs: the fractions depend on the route, so they
need an install-time calibration (load one side, read the other); and a rev A strip is
standalone and knows only its own six ways, so the total would have to come from the other strip
through Home Assistant. The yoke has the total on one board. Not worth it for 0.05 A.

**A shield.** A steel or mu-metal plate between the cable and the end sensor. JLC does not place
one, so it is a hand-fitted part. Soft iron near a Hall sensor also bends the sensor's own field
and adds hysteresis, which trades a small load-dependent error for a gain and offset change. No
part is proposed.

**A different sensor.** Not needed: the part is already differential. No alternative was priced.
For the record, the CC6905S8-3FB030 was $0.5603 at 30+ and $0.6387 at 10+, 4,939 in stock, on
LCSC on 2026-10-10 (C41421490).

## Verifying it

The check the yoke note already lists settles the one thing the datasheet leaves open. On the
first assembled strip, on the bench: pull every fuse on the strip's side, load the other side to
a known 30 A or more, lay the feed sideways over the strip's end, and read the end way. A
differential part should move by under 0.015 A at 30 A, which is 0.6 mV and about one ADC step
(assumed 12 bits), so the test is really "does it move at all". A single-Hall part would move by about 0.3 A. If it moves by
more than about 0.03 A at 30 A, reopen this note.

## Not verified

- The CC6905's coupling factor, Hall plate spacing, plate axis and matching. None is published.
  The differential figures use Allegro's ranges for its own SOIC-8 parts.
- Cross Chip's own site was not read. The datasheet is the rev1.3 copy LCSC serves; a later
  revision may exist.
- The cable's height and the lug's shape are assumptions carried from the yoke note. Lowering
  the cable centre from 22.4 to 16 raises the sideways figure from 9.4 to 11.5 G (calc); raising
  it to 28 drops it to 6.6 G.
- The panel's own positive bus. It carries the same total along the centreline, 46 mm from every
  sensor, whichever way the feed leaves. Taken as a line at sensor height carrying the full
  100 A, it puts up to about 2.5–2.9 G normal on each sensor (calc, on assumed geometry; the
  bus's real shape and height are not in the CAD note). On a differential part that is under
  0.01 A. It would have been about 0.3 A on a single-Hall part.
- A neighbouring way's own current runs parallel to each sensor's column, 13.36 mm away in the
  same plane. As a long straight line at 30 A it puts up to 4.5 G and 0.34 G/mm on the next
  sensor (calc, an upper bound), about 0.04 A by the same sum. That is the same size as the feed
  cable's worst case and is outside this ticket.
