Chapter 1 · on the desk

Flash it

Over USB-C, from a Mac, before it goes anywhere near the panel.

1
Flash · step 1 of 3

Install ESPHome and fill in the secrets.

brew install uv
uv tool install --python 3.13 esphome
uv tool update-shell

git clone https://github.com/XDGFX/blade-reader.git
cd blade-reader/firmware
cp secrets.example.yaml secrets.yaml
openssl rand -base64 32     # paste the result in as api_key

In secrets.yaml: your Wi-Fi (2.4 GHz only), a web login for the yoke's page, and a fallback password of eight characters or more.

Keep a copy of secrets.yaml in your password manager: without its key, updates go back over USB.

2
Flash · step 2 of 3

Plug it in and flash. A data cable into the socket on the chin; no buttons to press.

{{art:desk}}
ls /dev/cu.usbmodem*
esphome run blade-reader.yaml --device /dev/cu.usbmodemXXXX

No port listed? The cable only carries power: try another.

3
Flash · step 3 of 3

Check it's on Wi-Fi. The log shows an IP address, then a reading from every way.

The green LED blinks while something's wrong, such as no Wi-Fi, and goes out once it's connected. Open blade-reader-a.local and log in with the web login. If macOS asks, allow your terminal on the local network; if the name won't open, use the IP address from the log.

Then prove an update over Wi-Fi while the cable is still in reach:

esphome run blade-reader.yaml --device blade-reader-a.local

A second yoke needs its own name, given before run every time, and is then blade-reader-b.local:

esphome -s name blade-reader-b -s friendly_name "Blade Reader B" run blade-reader.yaml
Chapter 2 · on the desk

Zero it

Each way's sensor reads a little even with no current. Its zero is that reading, and every reading after has it taken off.

1
Zero · step 1 of 1

Leave it on USB for a minute. With nothing on its ways, all 12 zero at once.

About {{s:ZERO_DEAD_MS}} seconds after it starts, the red LED blinks fast for {{s:ZERO_MEAN_MS}} seconds while the zeros are taken, then goes out. It's done when Not Zeroed reads None on the yoke's page.

The zeros are saved straight away. They survive updates and the move to the panel; only a factory reset clears them.

Chapter 3 · at the panel

Install it

The yoke wraps the panel's positive end, ring face out and parts face towards the bulkhead. It needs 16.7 mm beside each long side, 22.7 mm where the Wi-Fi antenna overhangs, and 13.5 mm past the positive end.

The drawings show the panel lying on a bench. On a bulkhead, Blue Sea's way up is positive end down, and then the silkscreen reads upright.

1
Install · step 1 of 9

Isolate the battery.

The feed has to come off, and the + stud is live until you do.

2
Install · step 2 of 9

Strip the positive end. Feed lug off the + stud, every circuit's ring lifted, all 12 way screws out.

{{art:strip}}

Keep each screw's lock washer.

3
Install · step 3 of 9

Lower the yoke on. All 12 fingers go into their pockets together.

{{art:lower}}

Handle it gently: the yoke is fragile until it's installed.

4
Install · step 4 of 9

Refit all 12 way screws, each on its lock washer, and tighten them before anything else goes on.

{{art:screws}}

They're the yoke's only fixing. There's no torque figure for them yet: do them up as firmly as the panel's own screws.

5
Install · step 5 of 9

Refit the feed lug, and check it stands clear of the chin.

{{art:lug}}

Look for a gap all the way across. Lugs vary: a flat one needs a washer about 2 mm thick under it.

A lug touching the chin shorts the feed to ground.

6
Install · step 6 of 9

Bolt each circuit's ring to its own way's nut, 1.0–1.2 N·m, with an M4 split spring washer under the bolt.

{{art:rings}}
{{art:ringAngle}}

The way numbers are on the fingers. Run each tongue straight out, within about 20° either way, and hold it there while you tighten.

M4 rings for 4–6 mm² cable at most. Tie the cables to the bulkhead within about 100 mm.

7
Install · step 7 of 9

Fit the ground wire: from the nut marked GND to the panel's negative bus.

The GND nut is on the arm beside way 12, next to the chin.

{{art:ground}}

Fit it before plugging anything into the USB socket or the PROG pads.

8
Install · step 8 of 9

Put the cover on and restore the battery.

{{art:cover}}
9
Install · step 9 of 9

Re-torque after the first warm run. The yoke settles under the screws.

After the first run under load, isolate the battery again and recheck every way screw and every ring's bolt.

Chapter 4 · when you need to

Redo one way's zero

For a way that reads a little with nothing on, or when the red LED blinks slowly after start-up: that means a live way has no zero yet.

1
Redo a zero · step 1 of 1

Pull its fuse for {{s:ZERO_DEAD_MS+ZERO_MEAN_MS}} seconds, then put it back.

{{art:pullFuse}}
{{art:zeroTimeline}}

The red LED goes steady when the way goes dead, blinks fast while the zero is taken, and goes steady again once it's saved. Put the fuse back after the blinking stops: back in sooner, and that attempt is dropped. It stays steady for {{s:BLOWN_CLEARS_AFTER_MS}} seconds after the fuse is back, then goes out.

Chapter 5

Home Assistant

The yoke is one device in Home Assistant, with all 12 ways on it.

1
Home Assistant · step 1 of 3

Add it. In Settings, Devices & services, it shows up under Discovered: Configure it.

When it asks for the encryption key, give it the api_key from secrets.yaml.

2
Home Assistant · step 2 of 3

Find the ways on the Blade Reader device.

Each wayWay 7: Current, Power, Energy, Live, Blown Fuse
The yokePanel Voltage, Fault Text, Not Zeroed, Temperature: …
ButtonsClear Faults, Restart, Factory Reset
3
Home Assistant · step 3 of 3

Name your ways in Home Assistant, such as "Way 7: Bilge pump".

The yoke keeps no names of its own: rename each way's entities there.

Chapter 6 · if it won't update

Recovery

When USB and Wi-Fi updates both fail, flash it through the PROG pads with an Espressif ESP-PROG.

1
Recovery · step 1 of 2

Clamp the ESP-PROG on the PROG pads with a 2×3, 2.54 mm pogo clamp, slid on from the chin's edge. Set its power jumper to 5 V.

{{art:progPads}}
ESP-PROGPad
ENEN
VDDVDD
GNDGND
IO0IO0
TXDRXD
RXDTXD

TXD and RXD swap; the rest go straight through. No button to hold: the ESP-PROG starts it in its bootloader.

Do this off the panel. On it, fit the ground wire first and run the Mac on battery.

2
Recovery · step 2 of 2

Flash it as before, through the ESP-PROG's serial port.

ls /dev/cu.usbserial*
esphome run blade-reader.yaml --device /dev/cu.usbserial-XXXX1

The ESP-PROG shows two ports; the serial one is the second.
